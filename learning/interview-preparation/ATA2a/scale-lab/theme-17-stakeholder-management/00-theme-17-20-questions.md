## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q01 — Conflicting Stakeholder Priorities

### Interview Question
How would you handle a situation where HR wants a standardized recruiting process but business leaders want exceptions?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR wanted global consistency while business leaders requested several recruiting exceptions.

**Task:** I needed to reconcile both needs without weakening the target architecture.

**Action:** I classified each exception by legal necessity, business value, risk, and process impact. I separated genuine requirements from preferences and proposed only justified variants.

**Result:** Stakeholders agreed on a governed global model with controlled exceptions.

### SmartRecruiters Example
I would use SmartRecruiters as the common recruiting platform and allow local or business variation only where a documented requirement exists.

### SME Probe
How do you prevent stakeholder preference from becoming a “requirement”?

## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q02 — Executive Disagreement

### Interview Question
Two executives disagree on the priority of a recruiting transformation. How would you resolve it?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** One executive prioritized candidate experience while another prioritized recruiting cost.

**Task:** I needed to create a decision framework rather than choose a stakeholder.

**Action:** I translated both positions into measurable business outcomes, compared impact, risk, effort, and strategic alignment, and presented decision options with trade-offs.

**Result:** The executives aligned around a balanced outcome-based priority.

### SmartRecruiters Example
SmartRecruiters metrics could support the comparison through candidate conversion, recruiter productivity, cycle time, cost, and quality indicators.

### SME Probe
Why is an outcome-based decision stronger than an opinion-based compromise?

## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q03 — Resistant Recruiters

### Interview Question
Recruiters resist adopting SmartRecruiters because they prefer the legacy ATS. How would you manage the stakeholders?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Experienced recruiters viewed the new platform as disruptive.

**Task:** I needed adoption without dismissing their concerns.

**Action:** I listened to specific pain points, demonstrated the future process, involved representative recruiters in validation, addressed legitimate gaps, and created role-specific enablement.

**Result:** Resistance shifted toward participation and adoption improved.

### SmartRecruiters Example
I would use real recruiter journeys in SmartRecruiters demonstrations rather than feature-based training alone.

### SME Probe
What is the difference between resistance and a legitimate design defect?

## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q04 — Hiring Manager Engagement

### Interview Question

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Recruiting requirements were being designed mainly by HR and technology teams.

**Task:** I needed business participation to ensure the solution reflected actual hiring decisions.

**Action:** I identified representative hiring managers, used decision-focused workshops, showed current-state pain points, and asked them to validate critical journeys.

**Result:** The target process reflected real business needs and stakeholder ownership increased.

### SmartRecruiters Example
Hiring-manager journeys in SmartRecruiters should be validated for requisition approvals, candidate review, interviews, and decision-making.

### SME Probe
Why should stakeholder workshops focus on decisions and journeys rather than screens?

## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q06 — Stakeholder Expecting Customization

### Interview Question
A senior stakeholder asks you to customize SmartRecruiters to reproduce a legacy recruiting process exactly. How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A stakeholder wanted the new platform to reproduce several legacy behaviors.

**Task:** I needed to challenge unnecessary customization constructively.

**Result:** The stakeholder accepted a simpler target process where the legacy behavior added no business value.

### SmartRecruiters Example

## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q07 — Scope Creep

### Interview Question
How would you manage recruiting requirements that keep expanding during implementation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Stakeholders continuously introduced new requirements.

**Task:** I needed to protect delivery while maintaining business engagement.

**Action:** I established scope criteria, categorized requests by value and urgency, assessed dependencies and risk, and moved lower-priority items into a governed backlog.

**Result:** The implementation remained focused while stakeholders retained visibility of future improvements.

### SmartRecruiters Example
New SmartRecruiters requests should be evaluated against the agreed recruit-to-select scope and target architecture.

### SME Probe
How do you distinguish legitimate scope change from uncontrolled scope creep?

## HR-ATA2A-B17-Q09 — Vendor Stakeholder Conflict

### Interview Question
SmartRecruiters support and your internal team disagree about the cause of an incident. How would you handle it?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The vendor believed the issue was configuration-related while the internal team suspected platform behavior.

**Task:** I needed evidence-based resolution rather than competing opinions.

**Action:** I established reproducible test cases, exchanged logs and timestamps, compared known-good and failing scenarios, and documented hypotheses and evidence.

**Result:** The teams aligned around the actual fault domain.

### SmartRecruiters Example
A structured vendor diagnostic package should focus on reproducibility and transaction evidence.

### SME Probe
Why is assigning blame early harmful to incident resolution?

# Theme 17 Completion Standard

A learner completes **ATA2a Theme 17 — Stakeholder Management** when they can:

- Resolve conflicting recruiting priorities.
- Facilitate evidence-based executive decisions.
- Engage recruiters and hiring managers in solution design.
- Balance global standards with local needs.
- Challenge unnecessary customization constructively.
- Control scope and stakeholder expectations.
- Manage escalations and vendor disagreements.
- Establish clear data and decision ownership.
- Balance security, usability, candidate experience, and business value.
- Build change champions and executive trust.
- Drive decisions when consensus is unavailable.
- Demonstrate stakeholder management as an architecture capability.

**Quality rule:** Every scenario must demonstrate Situation → Task → Action → Result, contain a distinct stakeholder-management decision, use **SmartRecruiters** as the primary platform example, remain separate from onboarding, avoid duplication with Themes 01–16, and end with an SME Probe.

**Scenario IDs:** HR-ATA2A-B17-Q01 → HR-ATA2A-B17-Q20

**Target achieved:** 20 unique scenario-based interview questions + 20 STAR answers.
