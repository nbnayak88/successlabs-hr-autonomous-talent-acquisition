## HR-ATA2B-B17-Q01 — Conflicting Stakeholder Priorities

### Interview Question
HR wants a highly controlled onboarding process while business leaders want a faster employee experience. How would you manage the conflict?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR and business leaders had different definitions of successful onboarding.
**Task:** I needed to align them around a common outcome.
**Action:** I translated both positions into measurable objectives, separated mandatory controls from discretionary steps, quantified cycle-time and compliance impact, and facilitated a decision based on business value.
**Result:** Stakeholders agreed on a balanced target state rather than optimizing one dimension at the expense of another.

### SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding Example
I would design the journey so mandatory compliance remains protected while unnecessary approvals, duplicate tasks, and administrative friction are removed.

### SME Probe
How do you prevent the loudest stakeholder from becoming the decision-maker?

## HR-ATA2B-B17-Q02 — Executive Expectation Management

### Interview Question
An executive expects a major onboarding transformation to be completed in an unrealistic timeline. How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The requested timeline did not match the scope and dependencies.
**Task:** I needed to protect the outcome without simply rejecting the executive request.
**Action:** I decomposed the transformation into MVP, critical capabilities, dependencies, risks, and later releases and presented evidence-based options.
**Result:** Leadership could choose a realistic path while preserving the strategic objective.

### SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding Example
I would separate essential employee readiness, compliance, security, and integrations from enhancements that can follow post-go-live.

### SME Probe
What makes an executive conversation credible rather than defensive?

## HR-ATA2B-B17-Q14 — Stakeholder Communication for Complex Issues

### Interview Question
How would you explain a complex onboarding integration problem to a non-technical HR executive?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A technical integration issue was delaying employee readiness.
**Task:** I needed to communicate the issue in business language.
**Action:** I explained the affected business journey, population, impact, cause at the appropriate abstraction level, immediate containment, recovery timeline, and decision required.
**Result:** The executive could make the required business decision without needing integration-level technical detail.

### SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding Example
Instead of leading with API errors, I would explain how the failure affects employee setup and Day-1 readiness.

### SME Probe
What technical detail should remain out of an executive summary?

# Theme 17 Completion Standard

A learner completes **ATA2b Theme 17 — Stakeholder Management** when they can:

- Align conflicting HR, business, IT, security, compliance, and employee-experience priorities.
- Manage executive expectations.
- Clarify ownership and decision rights.
- Handle global/local resistance.
- Improve manager and new-hire adoption.
- Facilitate architecture and requirements decisions.
- Communicate complex technical problems in business language.
- Manage vendors and escalations.
- Facilitate cross-functional workshops.
- Negotiate scope and customization.
- Lead cross-functional production crises.
- Provide evidence-based executive decision support.
- Build trust as an enterprise onboarding advisor.
