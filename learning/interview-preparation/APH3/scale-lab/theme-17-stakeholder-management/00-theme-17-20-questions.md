## Q01 — How would you align HR and IT when they have different priorities?

### Interview Question
HR wants rapid Performance & Goals transformation while IT prioritizes stability and standardization. How would you align them?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR wanted a faster transformation while IT was concerned about complexity, risk, and supportability.

**Task:** I needed to create a shared decision framework rather than allow the teams to argue from their own perspectives.

**Action:** I translated both positions into business outcomes, risks, dependencies, and measurable criteria. I presented solution options with trade-offs and facilitated agreement around the target outcome.

**Result:** HR and IT moved from competing priorities to a jointly understood solution decision.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would compare standard SuccessFactors capability, customization, implementation effort, employee experience, security, and long-term support implications.

### SME Probe
How do you turn stakeholder disagreement into an architecture decision?

## Q02 — How would you handle a senior executive who rejects your recommendation?

### Interview Question
An executive strongly disagrees with your proposed Performance & Goals architecture. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A senior stakeholder preferred an approach that I believed created unnecessary complexity.

**Task:** I needed to influence the decision without making the disagreement personal.

**Action:** I clarified the executive's underlying business objective, tested my assumptions, presented evidence and alternatives, and made trade-offs explicit. If the stakeholder still chose another option, I documented the decision and its implications.

**Result:** The relationship remained constructive and the decision became transparent and governed.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would show the impact of standard versus customized Performance Form design on user experience, maintainability, upgradeability, and business value.

### SME Probe
What do you do when the stakeholder makes a decision you disagree with?

## Q03 — How would you manage conflicting requirements from global and local HR teams?

### Interview Question
Global HR wants one Performance & Goals process while local HR teams demand different processes. How would you manage the conflict?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Global and local stakeholders had competing requirements.

**Task:** I needed to protect enterprise consistency while recognizing legitimate local needs.

**Action:** I separated mandatory global principles from local requirements, assessed business and regulatory justification, and created a governed model for controlled variation.

**Result:** Stakeholders gained clarity on what was standardized and where local variation was justified.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would establish a common Goal Plan and Performance Form architecture with approved local variations where required.

### SME Probe
How do you distinguish a regulatory requirement from a local preference?

## Q04 — How would you identify the right stakeholders for a Performance & Goals transformation?

### Interview Question
Who should be involved when designing a global performance-management solution?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The program initially involved mainly HR and IT stakeholders.

**Task:** I needed to identify everyone who could influence or be affected by the solution.

**Action:** I mapped stakeholders across executives, HR process owners, employees, managers, HR operations, HR technology, security, data, analytics, integration, change, and regional representatives. I assessed their influence and interests and defined appropriate engagement.

**Result:** The program reduced blind spots and created broader ownership of the target solution.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would include performance-process owners, HR operations, Employee Central owners, security, analytics, integration, managers, employee representatives, and regional HR teams as appropriate.

### SME Probe
Why is an employee a stakeholder even if they do not configure the system?

## Q05 — How would you handle a stakeholder who keeps adding requirements?

### Interview Question
A business stakeholder continuously adds new requirements to the Performance Form. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Requirements were expanding and increasing form complexity.

**Task:** I needed to protect scope and solution quality without damaging the stakeholder relationship.

**Action:** I asked each new requirement to identify the business outcome, user population, decision supported, urgency, dependencies, and measurable value. I prioritized it against agreed criteria and made trade-offs visible.

**Result:** Requirement growth became governed rather than automatically accepted.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Additional form fields, competencies, ratings, or workflow steps would be evaluated against performance outcomes and user effort.

### SME Probe
What question should every new requirement answer?

## Q06 — How would you handle disagreement between HR and employees about performance design?

### Interview Question
HR wants more controls and documentation while employees want a simpler performance experience. How would you mediate?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Different stakeholder groups defined “good performance management” differently.

**Task:** I needed to create a solution that protected business governance while maintaining employee trust and usability.

**Action:** I identified the outcomes each group cared about, removed assumptions, mapped essential controls, and explored simpler ways to achieve the same objectives.

**Result:** The design retained necessary governance while reducing unnecessary employee burden.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would simplify Performance Forms while retaining evidence, ratings, feedback, and controls required for meaningful decisions.

### SME Probe

## Q07 — How would you manage an influential stakeholder who bypasses governance?

### Interview Question
A senior HR leader asks the team to make a production Performance & Goals change without the normal approval process. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A senior stakeholder requested an urgent change outside established governance.

**Task:** I needed to respect urgency without normalizing uncontrolled production changes.

**Action:** I clarified the business impact and urgency, explained the risk of bypassing governance, and proposed an expedited or emergency path with appropriate approvals and evidence.

**Result:** The business need was addressed while preserving governance integrity.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
A production change to a Goal Plan, Performance Form, rating scale, or permission would follow the approved emergency-change process when genuinely urgent.

### SME Probe
How do you say “no” to a senior stakeholder without becoming obstructive?

## Q08 — How would you build trust with HR stakeholders who see you as an IT person?

### Interview Question
How would you establish credibility as an HR technology architect with business stakeholders?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR stakeholders initially viewed architecture as technical rather than business-oriented.

**Task:** I needed to demonstrate that I understood HR outcomes and not just the product.

**Action:** I began with HR strategy, performance philosophy, employee and manager journeys, process pain points, and measurable business outcomes. I then connected those needs to SuccessFactors capability.

**Result:** Stakeholders began engaging with architecture as a business decision discipline rather than a technical gate.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would start with performance outcomes and process design before discussing Goal Plans, Performance Forms, route maps, or configuration.

### SME Probe

### Interview Question

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The stakeholder equated transformation with reproducing the existing process on a new platform.

**Task:** I needed to challenge the assumption constructively.

**Action:** I separated business outcomes from legacy implementation, demonstrated standard capabilities, identified process improvements, and presented the cost and risk of unnecessary replication.

**Result:** The stakeholder could evaluate the transformation based on outcomes rather than familiarity.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example

### SME Probe

## Q10 — How would you resolve stakeholder conflict over a rating methodology?

### Interview Question
Executives, HR, and business leaders disagree on the performance-rating scale. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Stakeholders had different views of how employee performance should be rated.

**Task:** I needed to facilitate a business decision before translating the methodology into technology.

**Action:** I clarified the purpose of ratings, decision use, calibration requirements, historical comparability, employee communication, and downstream dependencies. I presented options and trade-offs for governance decision.

**Result:** The rating methodology became an explicit business decision rather than an accidental configuration choice.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Once approved, the rating methodology would be reflected consistently in SuccessFactors rating scales and Performance Forms.

### SME Probe
Why should an architect avoid choosing a rating scale simply because the product supports it?

## Q13 — How would you handle a stakeholder who does not attend critical workshops?

### Interview Question
A key HR decision-maker repeatedly misses design workshops and later rejects decisions. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Important decisions were delayed because a critical stakeholder was not engaged consistently.

**Task:** I needed to restore ownership and avoid endless redesign.

**Action:** I clarified decision rights, provided concise pre-reads, established explicit decision deadlines, and obtained formal confirmation through the agreed governance channel. I avoided assuming silence meant approval.

**Result:** Decision accountability improved and late-stage objections decreased.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Major decisions on rating scales, Goal Plans, Performance Forms, and performance philosophy would have named business owners and documented approvals.

### SME Probe
Why is silence not stakeholder approval?

## Q17 — How would you manage stakeholder expectations after a major production issue?

### Interview Question
A Performance & Goals incident affects thousands of employees. How would you maintain executive trust?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A high-impact production issue created significant stakeholder concern.

**Task:** I needed to maintain confidence while the technical team investigated.

**Action:** I communicated verified facts, affected scope, business impact, current actions, decision points, and next updates. I avoided speculation and clearly separated known information from hypotheses.

**Result:** Stakeholders remained informed and trusted the response even before the technical root cause was fully known.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would communicate the affected Performance Forms or Goal Plans, business populations, workaround if available, recovery progress, and preventive actions.

### SME Probe
