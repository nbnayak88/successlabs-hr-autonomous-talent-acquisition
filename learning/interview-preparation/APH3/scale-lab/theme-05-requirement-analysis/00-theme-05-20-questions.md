# APH3 — Theme 05: Requirement Analysis

## 20 Scenario-Based Interview Questions & STAR Answers

**Stream:** APH3 — Applied SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals  
**Theme:** 05 — Requirement Analysis  
**Target:** 20 unique scenarios  
**Answer method:** STAR — Situation → Task → Action → Result  
**Product posture:** HCM-first, requirement-led, architecture-aware

> **Boundary:** APH3 covers Performance & Goals requirements. Employee Central remains the employee/organizational foundation; AGL4 owns Succession & Development; ARP5 owns Compensation & Variable Pay; ALM6 owns Learning.

## Requirement Discovery Spine

**Business Outcome → Stakeholder Need → Current Process → Pain Point → Requirement → Business Rule → Priority → Acceptance Criteria → Product Fit → Solution Decision**

---

## Q01 — Discovering the Real Requirement

### Interview Question
A client says, “We need a better performance form.” How would you determine what they actually need?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The stated requirement focused on the form, but the underlying process had broader problems.

**Task:** I needed to discover the business requirement behind the technology request.

**Action:** I interviewed employees, managers and HR, mapped the current performance journey, identified pain points and converted them into measurable business requirements before discussing configuration.

**Result:** The solution addressed the performance process rather than merely redesigning a digital form.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Assess the full Goal and Performance Management process before selecting form configuration.

### SME Probe
What questions would you ask before showing the client a product feature?

## Q02 — Requirement versus Solution

### Interview Question
A stakeholder specifically requests a new field on the performance form. How would you assess whether it is necessary?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR requested an additional field based on an existing spreadsheet.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether the field represented a genuine business requirement.

**Action:** I asked what decision the field supported, who used it, how often it changed and whether existing data already provided the answer. I then assessed its value against process complexity.

**Result:** Only information with a clear business purpose was included.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Evaluate whether a requested form field is required before adding it to a Performance Management template.

### SME Probe
What makes a field valuable rather than merely familiar?

---

## Q03 — Stakeholder Requirement Conflict

### Interview Question
Employees want a simple performance process, while HR wants extensive controls and data capture. How would you reconcile the requirements?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Stakeholders had conflicting expectations about process depth.

**Task:** I needed to create a requirement set that balanced governance and experience.

**Action:** I separated mandatory controls from preferences, quantified the impact of each requirement and prioritized requirements based on business risk, employee value and compliance need.

**Result:** The future process retained necessary controls without becoming unnecessarily complex.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use role-specific sections and controlled configuration to balance employee and HR requirements.

### SME Probe
Who should make the final decision when stakeholder requirements conflict?

## Q04 — Global Requirement Gathering

### Interview Question
A global enterprise has different performance practices across regions. How would you gather requirements without creating 20 separate solutions?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Regional teams presented many local requirements.

**Task:** I needed to distinguish genuine local needs from historical preferences.

**Action:** I used a common requirement template, classified requirements as global, local, regulatory or legacy, and identified reusable process patterns before approving variations.

**Result:** Requirement discovery produced a controlled global model with justified local differences.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Assess global Goal and Performance Management requirements before creating regional templates.

### SME Probe
How would you prove that a local requirement is genuinely necessary?

## Q05 — Requirement Prioritization

### Interview Question
The client has 100 requested performance-management features but limited implementation time. How would you prioritize them?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The backlog was much larger than the implementation capacity.

**Task:** I needed to focus the release on high-value requirements.

**Action:** I categorized requirements by business value, employee impact, risk, compliance necessity, dependency and implementation effort. Mandatory and high-value capabilities formed the core release; lower-value items moved to a roadmap.

**Result:** The team delivered a focused solution without treating every request as equally important.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Prioritize standard Performance & Goals capabilities before optional enhancements.

### SME Probe
Would you prioritize compliance over employee experience in every situation?

---

## Q06 — Acceptance Criteria

### Interview Question
How would you write acceptance criteria for a performance-cycle requirement?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Requirements were written as broad statements such as “managers must complete reviews.”

**Task:** I needed to make requirements testable.

**Action:** I defined the actor, condition, expected behavior, business rule and measurable outcome, then validated the criteria with business owners.

**Result:** Requirements could be traced directly into configuration, testing and sign-off.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Create acceptance criteria around goal creation, review, routing, permissions and completion states.

### SME Probe
What makes an acceptance criterion objectively testable?

## Q07 — Requirement Traceability

### Interview Question
How would you ensure every major performance requirement is reflected in the final solution?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Previous projects lost requirements between workshops and implementation.

**Task:** I needed to establish end-to-end traceability.

**Action:** I created a requirement-to-design-to-configuration-to-test traceability model and assigned ownership for unresolved requirements.

**Result:** Business stakeholders could see how their requirements were addressed and testing became more targeted.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Trace requirements through Performance Management configuration, permissions, workflows and test scenarios.

### SME Probe
What would you do with a requirement that cannot be traced to any business outcome?

## Q08 — Identifying Hidden Business Rules

### Interview Question
A stakeholder says, “Managers should be able to change goals anytime.” What would you clarify?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The request sounded simple but could affect performance fairness.

**Task:** I needed to expose the hidden business rules.

**Action:** I asked who can change goals, when, under what conditions, whether approval is required, what evidence is retained and how changes affect ratings.

**Result:** The requirement became a governed goal-change process rather than an unrestricted edit capability.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Define goal-change permissions and governance before configuring Goal Management behavior.

### SME Probe
Why are business rules often more important than the requested feature?

## Q09 — Requirements for Different Employee Populations

### Interview Question
Sales employees, engineers and frontline workers have different performance measures. How would you gather requirements?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A single performance model did not represent all work types equally.

**Task:** I needed to identify common requirements and legitimate role-specific needs.

**Action:** I created a common enterprise performance framework and conducted targeted discovery for each population, separating universal requirements from role-specific measures.

**Result:** The design supported meaningful performance assessment without uncontrolled fragmentation.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use controlled goal plans, competencies and performance-form variations for justified employee populations.

### SME Probe
When does role-specific configuration become excessive complexity?

## Q10 — Requirement for Continuous Feedback

### Interview Question
The CHRO says, “We need continuous feedback.” What requirements would you derive from that statement?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The request was strategic but too broad to configure directly.

**Task:** I needed to translate it into actionable requirements.

**Action:** I defined who gives feedback, when, about what, whether feedback is private or visible, how it is recorded, how it relates to formal reviews and what success looks like.

**Result:** A broad transformation objective became a clear set of process and technology requirements.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Translate continuous-performance objectives into check-in, feedback and evidence requirements.

### SME Probe
What would make “continuous feedback” a measurable requirement?

## Q11 — Requirement for Calibration

### Interview Question
Leadership asks for “calibration.” What would you clarify before designing the solution?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Different leaders had different interpretations of calibration.

**Task:** I needed to establish the intended business outcome.

**Action:** I clarified the population, rating data, decision rights, participants, evidence, timing and whether calibration meant consistency review or forced distribution.

**Result:** The requirement became precise enough to design and govern.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Translate the agreed calibration process into appropriate SuccessFactors calibration capabilities and governance.

### SME Probe
Why should “calibration” never be accepted as a complete requirement by itself?

## Q12 — Requirement for Performance Analytics

### Interview Question
HR wants “better performance analytics.” How would you turn this into requirements?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The request lacked defined decisions or measures.

**Task:** I needed to identify the analytical questions first.

**Action:** I asked which decisions HR and leaders wanted to make, then defined required dimensions, metrics, populations, time periods and access requirements.

**Result:** Analytics requirements became decision-led rather than dashboard-led.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Define reporting requirements around goals, ratings, feedback, completion and organizational context.

### SME Probe
Why should analytics requirements begin with decisions rather than charts?

## Q13 — Regulatory and Privacy Requirements

### Interview Question
A region has additional privacy requirements for performance information. How would you incorporate them?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance data included sensitive employee information subject to local controls.

**Task:** I needed to capture privacy requirements before solution design.

**Action:** I identified data categories, legitimate users, retention expectations, visibility rules and required controls, then translated them into security and process requirements.

**Result:** Privacy requirements became explicit solution constraints rather than late-stage technical surprises.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Assess Role-Based Permissions, form visibility and access requirements for sensitive performance information.

## Q14 — Requirement Change During Implementation

### Interview Question
A senior executive introduces a new performance requirement halfway through implementation. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A late requirement threatened the agreed implementation scope.

**Task:** I needed to assess the change without automatically accepting or rejecting it.

**Action:** I evaluated business value, urgency, dependencies, configuration impact, testing impact and release risk. I presented options and obtained a governed scope decision.

**Result:** The organization made an informed change decision instead of allowing uncontrolled scope expansion.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Assess whether the requested change affects Goal Plans, performance forms, workflow, permissions or reporting.

### SME Probe
Who owns the decision to accept a late requirement?

## Q15 — Legacy Requirement Challenge

### Interview Question
The client says, “We have always done it this way, so the system must support it.” How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Several requirements were based entirely on legacy operating practices.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether the legacy behavior still created business value.

**Action:** I asked what outcome the legacy step supported, assessed risk if removed and compared it with standard SuccessFactors capabilities and the future-state process.

**Result:** Some legacy requirements were eliminated, reducing unnecessary complexity.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use fit-to-standard analysis for Goal and Performance Management before recreating legacy processes.

### SME Probe
How do you challenge legacy requirements without damaging stakeholder trust?

## Q16 — Requirement Dependencies

### Interview Question
A performance requirement depends on employee hierarchy information from Employee Central. How would you capture the dependency?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The performance requirement could not function reliably without accurate organizational data.

**Task:** I needed to make the dependency explicit.

**Action:** I documented the source system, required attributes, timing, ownership, data quality expectations and failure-handling approach.

**Result:** The dependency became visible to both functional and technical teams.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Document Employee Central dependencies for manager, employee and organizational context used by Performance & Goals.

### SME Probe
What happens when a requirement is technically valid but its upstream data dependency is not reliable?

## Q17 — Requirement Feasibility

### Interview Question
A stakeholder requests a highly customized performance workflow. How would you assess feasibility?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The requested workflow differed significantly from the standard process.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether it was achievable and sustainable.

**Action:** I decomposed the requirement into business rules, compared each rule with standard capabilities, assessed configuration options, integration/extension needs, maintenance impact and user experience.

**Result:** The client received a clear fit-gap decision rather than an unsupported promise.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Perform fit-to-standard analysis across forms, route maps, permissions, Goal Management and continuous performance capabilities.

### SME Probe
When does a functional requirement become an architectural constraint?

## Q18 — Requirement Conflict with Product Standard

### Interview Question
A business requirement conflicts with a standard SuccessFactors capability. How would you handle the conversation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The client wanted behavior that would require substantial deviation from standard product behavior.

**Task:** I needed to protect the SaaS operating model while addressing the business need.

**Action:** I clarified the outcome, explored process redesign, evaluated standard alternatives and only considered extensions or integrations where the business value justified the additional complexity.

**Result:** The organization made a conscious fit-to-standard decision rather than defaulting to customization.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use standard Performance & Goals capabilities first and document justified gaps explicitly.

### SME Probe
What evidence would justify accepting a product gap?

## Q19 — Requirement Sign-Off

### Interview Question
Business stakeholders disagree about whether the requirements are complete. How would you achieve sign-off?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Workshops produced conflicting interpretations of the future-state process.

**Task:** I needed to establish a reliable baseline.

**Action:** I consolidated requirements, removed duplicates, clarified assumptions and dependencies, linked requirements to business outcomes and acceptance criteria, and obtained accountable business-owner approval.

**Result:** The implementation team had a controlled baseline for design and testing.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Baseline requirements for Goal Management, Performance Management, permissions, workflow, analytics and integrations.

## Q20 — Architect-Level Requirement Analysis

### Interview Question
What distinguishes an architect's requirement-analysis approach from a functional consultant's feature checklist?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization was collecting product features instead of understanding the transformation problem.

**Task:** I needed to elevate requirement analysis to an architecture level.

**Action:** I connected business strategy, operating model, employee experience, process, information, application, integration, security, analytics and measurable outcomes. I challenged assumptions, prioritized capabilities and defined clear boundaries between HCM domains.

**Result:** Requirements became a foundation for enterprise performance transformation rather than a list of system features.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Analyze APH3 requirements across Goal Management, Performance Management, Continuous Performance, Employee Central dependencies, analytics, security and adjacent HCM processes.

### SME Probe
What requirement would you reject even if the product could technically support it?
