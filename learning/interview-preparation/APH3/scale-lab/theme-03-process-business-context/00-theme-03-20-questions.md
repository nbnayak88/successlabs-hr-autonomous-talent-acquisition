## Q01 — From Strategy to Individual Goals

### Interview Question
A business unit has a new growth strategy, but employees continue working against last year's objectives. How would you redesign the performance process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Strategic priorities had changed, while individual objectives remained largely unchanged.

**Task:** I needed to connect enterprise priorities to the employee performance cycle.

**Action:** I translated strategic priorities into measurable organizational goals, aligned team objectives, defined individual contributions and established governance for goal quality and periodic review.

**Result:** Employees could see how their objectives contributed to the current business strategy, improving strategic alignment.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use Goal Management to cascade or align organizational, team and individual goals within the performance process.

### SME Probe
How would you measure whether goal alignment actually improved business execution?

## Q02 — Annual Appraisal to Continuous Performance

### Interview Question
A client wants to replace its annual appraisal culture with continuous performance management. What process changes would you recommend?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers and employees treated performance as a once-a-year event.

**Task:** I needed to create a continuous performance rhythm without eliminating formal governance.

**Action:** I introduced regular check-ins, ongoing feedback, evidence capture and manager conversations, while retaining formal review milestones for ratings and accountability.

**Result:** Performance became an ongoing management practice rather than an annual administrative exercise.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use Continuous Performance Management capabilities alongside formal Performance Management processes.

### SME Probe
What would you change in manager behavior, not just system configuration?

## Q03 — Goal Quality

### Interview Question
Many employee goals are vague statements such as “improve customer service.” How would you improve goal quality?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Goals were difficult to assess because they lacked measurable outcomes.

**Task:** I needed to improve goal quality before performance evaluation.

**Action:** I introduced goal-writing guidance covering outcome, measure, target, timeframe and ownership. Managers reviewed goals early in the cycle rather than waiting for year-end.

**Result:** Performance discussions were based on clearer and more measurable commitments.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use Goal Management instructions, fields, weighting and review practices to improve goal quality.

### SME Probe
Why should goal quality be treated as a process-control issue rather than only an employee-writing issue?

## Q04 — Manager Accountability

### Interview Question
Managers consistently delay goal setting and performance reviews. How would you address this?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Delayed manager actions were creating incomplete performance cycles.

**Task:** I needed to improve manager accountability.

**Action:** I defined process ownership, milestone dates, escalation paths and management reporting. I also simplified unnecessary process steps and coached managers on the business purpose behind each milestone.

**Result:** Completion improved because accountability was embedded into the operating process rather than left to individual reminders.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use workflow, form status and reporting capabilities to monitor performance-cycle progress.

### SME Probe
What performance-process metrics would you put on an HR leadership dashboard?

## Q05 — Employee Experience

### Interview Question
Employees complain that the performance process is complicated and administrative. How would you redesign it?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees viewed performance reviews as compliance activity rather than meaningful conversations.

**Task:** I needed to simplify the experience while preserving required controls.

**Action:** I mapped the employee journey, removed redundant data capture, simplified instructions and separated essential performance evidence from administrative content.

**Result:** The process became easier to understand and more focused on meaningful performance conversations.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Design streamlined Goal and Performance Management experiences with clear sections, instructions and role-specific actions.

### SME Probe
How would you determine whether a performance process is genuinely employee-centric?

## Q06 — Manager and Employee Roles

### Interview Question
A client has confusion about who owns goals, feedback and final ratings. How would you clarify the operating model?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance responsibilities were unclear, causing delays and disagreements.

**Task:** I needed to establish explicit ownership.

**Action:** I mapped responsibilities across employee, manager, HR and leadership for goal creation, review, feedback, evidence, ratings and approvals.

**Result:** Process accountability improved and system permissions could be designed around a clearer operating model.

## Q07 — Mid-Year Check-In

### Interview Question
A company wants a formal mid-year performance conversation but does not want to duplicate the annual review. How would you design the process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The annual review was overloaded because all performance discussions happened at year-end.

**Task:** I needed to introduce a meaningful mid-cycle intervention.

**Action:** I defined the mid-year conversation around progress, obstacles, goal changes, feedback and support rather than final ratings. The annual review remained the formal assessment point.

**Result:** Managers could address performance risks earlier without creating a second annual appraisal.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use continuous performance/check-in capabilities and appropriate performance-process milestones.

### SME Probe
What information should deliberately not become a premature final rating?

## Q08 — Goal Changes After Business Disruption

### Interview Question
A major market disruption makes several employee goals unrealistic. How should the business process respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** External conditions changed materially after goals had been approved.

**Task:** I needed to preserve fairness while keeping employees aligned to current priorities.

**Action:** I established a governed goal-review process, assessed the original commitment and changed circumstances, documented approved changes and retained historical evidence.

**Result:** Employees were evaluated against realistic commitments without losing accountability for the original business context.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use governed goal modification processes with appropriate permissions and documentation.

### SME Probe
What evidence should remain visible when a goal is changed?

## Q09 — Performance Evidence

### Interview Question
Managers often base ratings on recent events rather than the complete performance cycle. How would you improve evidence quality?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Year-end ratings were affected by recency bias.

**Task:** I needed to encourage evidence-based performance assessment.

**Action:** I introduced regular feedback and evidence capture throughout the cycle, encouraged managers to document outcomes and examples, and used structured review guidance before final ratings.

**Result:** Performance discussions became more balanced and less dependent on the most recent event.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use continuous feedback and performance-form comments/evidence to support the formal review.

### SME Probe
How would you identify recency bias in performance data?

## Q10 — Rating Consistency

### Interview Question
Two managers use the same rating scale but interpret “Exceeds Expectations” very differently. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Rating definitions existed, but managerial interpretation varied.

**Task:** I needed to improve rating consistency.

**Action:** I clarified rating definitions with behavioral and outcome examples, trained managers and introduced calibration conversations focused on evidence.

**Result:** Ratings became more comparable across teams.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Combine standardized rating scales, manager guidance and calibration processes.

### SME Probe

## Q11 — Calibration as a Business Process

### Interview Question
Executives want calibration, but managers fear that it will override their professional judgment. How would you position it?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers viewed calibration as a centralized rating-control mechanism.

**Task:** I needed to establish calibration as a consistency and decision-quality process.

**Action:** I positioned calibration as an evidence-based discussion of rating patterns, clarified decision rights and prohibited arbitrary forced distributions.

**Result:** Managers retained accountability while leadership gained greater confidence in rating consistency.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use calibration capabilities where available and appropriate to support structured performance-rating discussions.

### SME Probe
What is the difference between calibration and forced ranking?

## Q12 — Performance and Development Boundary

### Interview Question
A performance review identifies a capability gap. Should APH3 own the employee's development plan?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance discussions were generating development needs.

**Task:** I needed to connect the signal to the correct HR process without creating overlapping ownership.

**Action:** I kept the performance assessment and development signal within the performance process, then handed the development requirement into the appropriate development/talent process owned by AGL4.

**Result:** Performance became a reliable input to development without blurring product and process ownership.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use performance evidence to identify development needs; AGL4 owns the broader Succession & Development process.

### SME Probe
Why is identifying a development need different from managing a development plan?

## Q13 — Performance and Compensation Boundary

### Interview Question
Managers ask whether a high performance rating should automatically produce a salary increase. How would you answer?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees assumed performance ratings directly determined compensation outcomes.

**Task:** I needed to clarify the relationship between the two processes.

**Action:** I explained that performance results can be an input into reward decisions, but compensation requires separate eligibility, budget, market and reward governance.

**Result:** The organization maintained a connected but controlled performance-to-reward process.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
APH3 produces performance outcomes; ARP5 owns Compensation & Variable Pay decisions and configuration.

### SME Probe
What other factors besides performance might influence compensation decisions?

## Q14 — Performance Data Quality

### Interview Question
HR discovers that many employees have incomplete goals before the review cycle begins. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Incomplete goals were threatening the quality of the upcoming performance cycle.

**Task:** I needed to correct the process before ratings were generated.

**Action:** I profiled incomplete records, identified root causes, assigned ownership for correction and established pre-cycle validation controls.

**Result:** The review population entered the cycle with better-quality performance data.

### Interview Question

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization measured only whether forms were completed.

**Task:** I needed to expand measurement from administrative completion to business effectiveness.

**Action:** I introduced metrics covering goal quality, completion, feedback frequency, manager participation, rating distribution, cycle time, employee experience and links to workforce outcomes.

## Q18 — Bias and Fairness

### Interview Question
Employees report that performance ratings differ systematically between teams. How would you investigate?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Rating patterns suggested possible inconsistency or bias.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether the issue came from process design, manager behavior, population differences or data.

**Action:** I analyzed rating distributions by relevant organizational dimensions, reviewed evidence standards, examined calibration practices and engaged HR governance before drawing conclusions.

**Result:** The organization gained an evidence-based view of rating fairness and could target the underlying causes.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use performance data, reporting and calibration processes to identify unusual rating patterns while respecting access controls.

### SME Probe
Why should analytics identify a potential bias signal rather than automatically label a manager as biased?

## Q19 — Process Failure During Review Cycle

### Interview Question
Halfway through the performance cycle, HR discovers that managers are not completing reviews consistently. How would you recover the process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Completion rates had fallen significantly during an active cycle.

**Task:** I needed to recover the cycle without creating unnecessary administrative disruption.

**Action:** I segmented the backlog, identified process bottlenecks, clarified manager actions, provided targeted support and established escalation reporting. I avoided changing core configuration unless evidence showed a configuration problem.

**Result:** The organization recovered the cycle while preserving continuity and avoiding unnecessary system changes.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Use form status, workflow information and reporting to identify stalled performance processes.

### SME Probe
How do you distinguish a technology defect from a process-adoption problem?

## Q20 — Designing the Performance Operating Model

### Interview Question
You are asked to architect the complete Performance & Goals operating model for a large enterprise. What would you design first?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization had a fragmented performance process with inconsistent practices, technology configuration and management behavior.

**Task:** I needed to create a coherent enterprise performance capability.

**Action:** I started with business strategy and performance philosophy, then designed goal architecture, employee and manager journeys, continuous feedback, formal review, rating governance, calibration, data ownership, technology configuration, analytics and improvement mechanisms. I explicitly defined boundaries with Employee Central, Succession & Development and Compensation.

**Result:** Performance & Goals became an integrated business capability rather than an isolated annual form process.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Architect the SuccessFactors Performance & Goals solution around the end-to-end performance operating model, using standard capabilities first and integrating with adjacent HCM capabilities through defined boundaries.

### SME Probe
What would make you reject a technically correct Performance & Goals design?
