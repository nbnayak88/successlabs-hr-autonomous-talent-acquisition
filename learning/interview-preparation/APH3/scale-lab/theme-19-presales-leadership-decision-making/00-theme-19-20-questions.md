**Stream:** APH3 — Applied SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals

## Q01 — How would you qualify a Performance & Goals opportunity?

### Interview Question
A client asks for a SuccessFactors Performance & Goals proposal. How would you determine whether the opportunity is worth pursuing?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The client requested a solution proposal before clearly defining its business problem.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether there was a genuine transformation opportunity and whether our proposed capability could create measurable value.

**Action:** I assessed business objectives, current-state pain points, scope, stakeholders, decision process, timeline, technology landscape, competitive context, delivery feasibility, and measurable success criteria.

**Result:** We could distinguish a strategic opportunity from a feature-led request and decide how much presales investment was justified.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would assess the client's performance philosophy, Goal Plans, Performance Forms, feedback processes, ratings, analytics, Employee Central dependencies, and adoption challenges.

### SME Probe
What makes an opportunity strategically attractive rather than merely large?

### Interview Question
How would you structure a proposal for a global Performance & Goals transformation?

**Situation:** The client wanted a proposal focused heavily on product functionality.

**Task:** I needed to reposition the proposal around business outcomes and architecture.

**Action:** I structured it around current-state challenges, target outcomes, business capabilities, future-state process, SuccessFactors solution architecture, data and integration, security, implementation approach, risks, adoption, measurable value, and roadmap.

**Result:** The proposal demonstrated how technology would enable transformation rather than simply listing features.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
The proposal would show how Goal Plans, Performance Forms, feedback, calibration-related processes, Employee Central data, analytics, security, and integration contribute to the target operating model.

### SME Probe
Why is architecture important in presales?

### Interview Question

### STAR Answer

**Action:** I evaluated countries, employee populations, performance-cycle variations, process complexity, templates, workflows, security, integrations, data migration, reporting, customization, testing, change management, and dependencies.

**Result:** We produced an evidence-based complexity range and clearly documented assumptions.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example

### SME Probe

## Q04 — How would you defend an architecture recommendation to a client?

### Interview Question
A client challenges your recommendation to use a standard SuccessFactors design. How would you defend it?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The client believed customization would better reproduce its legacy process.

**Task:** I needed to demonstrate why the proposed architecture was more sustainable.

**Action:** I compared both options against business fit, user experience, lifecycle complexity, maintainability, upgrade impact, cost, risk, and future scalability. I used evidence rather than asserting that standardization was inherently better.

**Result:** The client could evaluate the recommendation transparently and understand the long-term implications.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would compare standard Goal Plan and Performance Form capabilities with the legacy requirements and identify only genuine gaps requiring alternative treatment.

### SME Probe
What evidence would make you change your recommendation?

## Q05 — How would you handle a commercial request that creates architecture risk?

### Interview Question
Sales asks you to promise a highly customized Performance & Goals solution to win the deal. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The proposed commercial commitment could create substantial delivery and lifecycle risk.

**Task:** I needed to protect both the client outcome and delivery credibility while supporting the sales objective.

**Action:** I quantified the risk, explained assumptions and consequences, proposed a safer alternative, and defined what could be committed versus what required discovery or validation.

**Result:** The commercial discussion became more realistic without sacrificing architectural integrity.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would avoid promising unsupported or unnecessarily customized performance behavior before confirming product capability and architecture impact.

### SME Probe
When should an architect say “no” in presales?

## Q06 — How would you prioritize competing client requirements during presales?

### Interview Question
A client has 100 Performance & Goals requirements but limited budget. How would you prioritize them?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The requirement backlog exceeded available implementation capacity.

**Task:** I needed to help leadership focus investment on the highest-value outcomes.

**Action:** I grouped requirements by business outcome, regulatory necessity, employee impact, strategic value, complexity, dependency, and risk. I separated must-have capabilities from preferences and roadmap items.

**Result:** The client obtained a defensible prioritization rather than arbitrary scope reduction.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Core performance-cycle capabilities would be separated from advanced analytics, optional workflow complexity, historical enhancements, and lower-value customizations.

### SME Probe
What should never be prioritized solely by the loudest stakeholder?

## Q07 — How would you handle an RFP question you cannot answer confidently?

### Interview Question
An RFP asks whether SuccessFactors can support a complex performance requirement, but you are not certain. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The answer could materially affect the proposal but required product validation.

**Task:** I needed to maintain credibility and avoid making an unsupported commitment.

**Action:** I documented the requirement precisely, identified the uncertainty, validated it through appropriate product evidence or specialists, and stated assumptions and qualification conditions in the proposal.

**Result:** The response remained credible while reducing delivery risk.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would validate complex Performance Form, Goal Plan, workflow, reporting, or integration behavior before marking the requirement fully supported.

### SME Probe
Why is “yes” without qualification dangerous in presales?

## Q08 — How would you present an architecture trade-off to a steering committee?

### Interview Question
A steering committee must choose between a faster implementation and a more scalable architecture. How would you guide the decision?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The options optimized different business priorities.

**Task:** I needed leadership to understand the trade-off and make an explicit decision.

**Action:** I quantified time, cost, risk, scalability, user impact, technical debt, and future migration implications. I recommended the option aligned to the strategic horizon and clearly stated the consequences of each choice.

**Result:** Leadership made an informed decision with ownership of the trade-off.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
A phased standard implementation could provide faster value while a more complex global design might provide greater long-term consistency.

### SME Probe
Should architects make trade-offs disappear or make them explicit?

## Q10 — How would you handle an executive asking for a guaranteed outcome?

### Interview Question
A CHRO asks you to guarantee a 30% improvement in performance-process efficiency. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The desired outcome was ambitious but depended on process, technology, adoption, and organizational behavior.

**Task:** I needed to avoid making an unsupported promise while maintaining confidence.

**Action:** I decomposed the outcome into measurable drivers, established a baseline, identified controllable and external factors, and proposed target ranges with measurement criteria.

**Result:** Leadership received a credible value hypothesis rather than an artificial guarantee.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
Efficiency could be measured through cycle completion time, manager effort, workflow exceptions, form completion, support volume, and adoption.

### SME Probe
How do you distinguish a business-value target from a sales promise?

## Q12 — How would you recover a presales proposal that has become too complex?

### Interview Question
The proposal has accumulated hundreds of requirements and customizations. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Proposal scope had expanded beyond the original transformation objective.

**Task:** I needed to restore strategic focus.

**Action:** I traced requirements back to business outcomes, identified duplicate or low-value requirements, separated mandatory controls from preferences, challenged legacy replication, and redesigned the scope into core, optional, and roadmap capabilities.

**Result:** The proposal became more deliverable, commercially credible, and easier to govern.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I would simplify unnecessary Performance Form fields, workflows, variants, and legacy behaviors that do not contribute to the target performance model.

## Q13 — How would you make an executive decision when data is incomplete?

### Interview Question
The client must decide on a Performance & Goals architecture, but several facts are still unknown. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Waiting for perfect information would delay an important decision.

**Task:** I needed to enable a responsible decision without hiding uncertainty.

**Action:** I separated known facts, assumptions, unknowns, reversible decisions, and irreversible decisions. I quantified uncertainty and recommended validation for high-impact unknowns.

**Result:** Leadership could proceed with controlled uncertainty rather than either guessing or stopping the program.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
I might proceed with an architectural direction while validating detailed form behavior or historical-data requirements through a prototype.

### SME Probe
What decisions should be delayed when information is incomplete?

## Q17 — How would you handle an executive who wants a decision immediately?

### Interview Question
An executive says, “I don't need another analysis. Just tell me what we should do.” How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The executive needed a fast recommendation under time pressure.

**Task:** I needed to provide clarity without hiding material risk.

**Action:** I summarized the objective, recommendation, two or three decisive reasons, material risk, assumption, and next action. I avoided unnecessary detail while preserving the critical decision context.

**Result:** The executive could act quickly with an informed understanding of the recommendation.

### SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example
