### HR-APH3-B01-Q01 — Performance Management as a Business Capability

**Interview Question:** How would you explain Performance & Goals as a business capability rather than an HR form?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** An organization viewed performance management primarily as an annual appraisal exercise.

**Task:** I needed to reposition it as a capability that connects employee contribution with business strategy.

**Action:** I mapped organizational objectives to team goals, individual goals, ongoing feedback, performance evidence, review conversations and development signals.

**Result:** Performance management became a continuous business-performance and employee-development capability rather than an annual compliance activity.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goals and performance forms can connect organizational priorities with individual objectives, competencies, feedback and review outcomes.

**SME Probe:** What business outcome should performance management ultimately improve?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q02 — Goals vs Performance

**Interview Question:** What is the difference between goal management and performance management?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client treated goal completion as equivalent to overall performance.

**Task:** I needed to clarify the distinction.

**Action:** I explained that goals represent expected outcomes, while performance management evaluates achievement, behaviors, competencies, contribution and development through a broader performance process.

**Result:** The client redesigned the process so goal achievement was one evidence source rather than the entire performance judgment.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans can capture objectives while performance forms provide a broader structured evaluation.

**SME Probe:** Can someone achieve all goals and still receive a lower performance assessment?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q03 — Cascading Organizational Goals

**Interview Question:** How would you design goal alignment from enterprise strategy to individual employees?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees had individual goals that were poorly connected to corporate priorities.

**Task:** I needed to improve strategic alignment.

**Action:** I established a goal hierarchy from enterprise priorities through business units, teams and individuals, while allowing appropriate role-specific objectives.

**Result:** Employees could see how their contribution supported broader business outcomes.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans can support organizational and individual goal alignment through cascading and aligned objectives.

**SME Probe:** What risk occurs when every employee goal is cascaded mechanically?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q04 — SMART Goals

**Interview Question:** How would you help managers create better performance goals?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Many goals were vague and difficult to evaluate objectively.

**Task:** I needed to improve goal quality.

**Action:** I coached managers to define specific outcomes, measurable indicators, realistic scope, relevant business contribution and clear time expectations.

**Result:** Performance discussions became more evidence-based and less subjective.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans can capture measurable objectives, milestones, weights and status information.

**SME Probe:** Is every important contribution measurable through a numeric KPI?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q05 — Continuous Performance

**Interview Question:** Why should performance management move beyond an annual review?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees received feedback only once a year, making performance correction slow.

**Task:** I needed to create a more continuous performance culture.

**Action:** I introduced regular check-ins, feedback, goal updates and coaching conversations throughout the performance cycle.

**Result:** Managers could address performance and development needs earlier.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Continuous performance capabilities can support ongoing feedback and conversations alongside formal performance forms.

**SME Probe:** What is the relationship between continuous feedback and the formal annual review?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q07 — Performance Ratings

**Interview Question:** What should a performance rating represent?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers used ratings inconsistently and sometimes treated them as personality judgments.

**Task:** I needed to establish a defensible rating philosophy.

**Action:** I aligned ratings to defined performance criteria, achievement evidence, competencies, behaviors and organizational expectations, supported by manager calibration.

**Result:** Ratings became more consistent and explainable.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance forms can capture ratings against defined competencies, goals and performance criteria.

**SME Probe:** What evidence should a manager provide when assigning a low performance rating?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q08 — Competencies

**Interview Question:** Why are competencies important in Performance & Goals?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client evaluated only what employees achieved and ignored how they achieved it.

**Task:** I needed to introduce behavioral and capability dimensions.

**Action:** I incorporated role-relevant competencies alongside goals and established clear behavioral expectations.

**Result:** Performance assessment reflected both outcomes and behaviors.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Competency sections in performance forms can complement goal achievement and provide a broader performance view.

**SME Probe:** How should competencies differ between an individual contributor and a people leader?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q09 — Goal Weighting

**Interview Question:** How would you determine whether performance goals should have different weights?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers gave every goal equal importance even when strategic priorities differed significantly.

**Task:** I needed to make performance evaluation reflect business priorities.

**Action:** I established weighting principles based on strategic importance, accountability and measurable business contribution.

**Result:** Performance outcomes better reflected what mattered most to the organization.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans can support weighted objectives where the performance philosophy requires differentiated importance.

**SME Probe:** What happens if a low-weight goal becomes critical because business priorities change?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q10 — Goal Changes During the Year

**Interview Question:** What would you do when business priorities change after employees have already set their goals?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Market conditions changed significantly halfway through the performance cycle.

**Task:** I needed to keep performance evaluation fair and aligned with current priorities.

**Action:** I introduced governed goal updates, documented changes and ensured managers considered the timing and context of changed objectives.

**Result:** Employees were evaluated against meaningful expectations rather than obsolete targets.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans can support updates to objectives while governance determines when and how changes affect evaluation.

**SME Probe:** How would you prevent goal changes from being used to manipulate performance ratings?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q11 — Performance Calibration

**Interview Question:** Why is calibration important in performance management?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Similar performance produced significantly different ratings across departments.

**Task:** I needed to improve consistency and fairness.

**Action:** I established calibration discussions using evidence, rating definitions, organizational standards and comparative context while avoiding forced ranking as a substitute for judgment.

**Result:** Managers developed greater consistency in applying performance standards.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Calibration capabilities can support structured review of ratings and distribution patterns before finalization.

**SME Probe:** What is the difference between calibration and forced distribution?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q13 — Performance and Compensation Boundary

**Interview Question:** How would you explain the relationship between performance and compensation without mixing the two domains?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client wanted performance ratings to automatically determine every compensation decision.

**Task:** I needed to clarify the architectural boundary.

**Action:** I established performance as an input that may inform reward processes, while compensation policy, calculations and reward decisions remained separately governed.

**Result:** The organization achieved integration without collapsing two distinct capabilities.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance outcomes can provide governed inputs to compensation processes, while ARP5 owns Compensation & Variable Pay.

### HR-APH3-B01-Q14 — Manager Accountability

**Interview Question:** How would you improve manager accountability for performance management?

### STAR Answer

**Task:** I needed to make manager behavior part of the operating model.

### HR-APH3-B01-Q15 — Bias in Performance Reviews

**Interview Question:** How would you address bias in performance assessments?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Analysis suggested differences in ratings that could not be explained by performance evidence alone.

**Task:** I needed to improve fairness without assuming intentional discrimination.

**Action:** I reviewed rating definitions, evidence requirements, calibration practices, manager training, demographic patterns and process design.

**Result:** The organization strengthened fair-performance practices and improved transparency.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Structured forms, competencies, evidence and calibration can support more consistent evaluation, but technology cannot eliminate human bias by itself.

**SME Probe:** What evidence would indicate a systemic performance-process bias?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q16 — Performance Process Governance

**Interview Question:** What governance is needed for an enterprise Performance & Goals process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Different business units had developed inconsistent performance cycles and rating practices.

**Task:** I needed to create enterprise consistency without eliminating legitimate business variation.

**Action:** I defined global principles for goal setting, rating scales, competencies, review cycles, security and governance while allowing controlled local variation.

**Result:** The organization gained comparable performance information and clearer governance.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Templates, rating scales, competencies, permissions and process calendars can be governed centrally with approved variations.

**SME Probe:** What should be standardized globally and what may remain local?

**Task:** I needed to improve information quality before expanding analytics.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal status, ratings, competencies and form completion data require consistent definitions and governance.
