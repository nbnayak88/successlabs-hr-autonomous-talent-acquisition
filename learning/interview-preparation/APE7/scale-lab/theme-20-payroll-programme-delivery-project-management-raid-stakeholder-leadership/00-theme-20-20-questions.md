# APE7 — Theme 20: Payroll Programme Delivery, Project Management, RAID & Stakeholder Leadership

**Purpose:** Practise leading payroll programmes through integrated planning, dependency management, RAID governance, stakeholder alignment, delivery assurance, and controlled decision-making.

**Practice guidance:** These STAR answers are illustrative models, not claims about personal experience. Adapt them to evidence you can substantiate. Validate product, country, regulatory, and delivery assumptions against the actual programme.

## Q01 — How do you establish an integrated payroll delivery plan?
### Interview Question
HR, payroll, integration, finance, security, and vendor teams each have separate plans. How do you create one credible delivery view?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Workstreams tracked progress independently, hiding cross-team dependencies.
- **Task:** Establish an integrated plan tied to payroll-critical milestones.
- **Action:** Align deliverables, dependencies, owners, entry/exit criteria, environments, test cycles, country calendars, and decision dates. Identify the critical path and review changes through a shared planning cadence.
- **Result:** Leadership can see the actual delivery path, cross-team constraints, and upcoming decisions.
### SAP Payroll Example
Link employee/time data readiness, payroll configuration, interfaces, finance posting, payment handoff, statutory reporting, and operational readiness.
### SME Probe
How do you validate that a reported milestone is complete rather than merely marked green?

## Q02 — How do you manage RAID effectively?
### Interview Question
The risk, assumption, issue, and dependency log contains hundreds of stale entries. How do you restore its value?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** The RAID log had become a repository rather than a decision tool.
- **Task:** Focus attention on material risks and actionable blockers.
- **Action:** Define entry criteria, owner, due date, impact, likelihood, mitigation, trigger, escalation threshold, and closure evidence. Review ageing and duplicates, escalate overdue high-severity items, and link risks to milestones and decisions.
- **Result:** RAID governance supports timely intervention and accountable closure.
### SAP Payroll Example
Track payroll input readiness, statutory interpretation, interface resilience, data defects, reconciliation, provider readiness, and cutover dependencies.
### SME Probe
What evidence is required before closing a risk that has not yet materialised?

## Q03 — How do you manage a critical-path dependency?
### Interview Question
The payroll test cycle depends on a finance interface that will arrive late. What do you do?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** A delayed dependency threatened an integrated test milestone.
- **Task:** Protect delivery assurance without hiding the impact.
- **Action:** Confirm the dependency and critical-path impact, assess alternatives, identify safe parallel work, agree a recovery owner and date, and define evidence needed to restore confidence. Escalate if the revised plan compromises a required control or deadline.
- **Result:** The team has a transparent recovery plan and an evidence-based decision on scope and timing.
### SAP Payroll Example
Assess whether payroll posting tests can progress with controlled stubs or mocks, while preserving end-to-end validation before release.
### SME Probe
When does a workaround create false confidence instead of reducing schedule risk?

## Q04 — How do you manage scope change?
### Interview Question
A senior stakeholder requests a new payroll feature shortly before a country rollout. How do you evaluate it?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** A late request competed with stabilisation and statutory readiness work.
- **Task:** Evaluate value and impact without allowing uncontrolled scope growth.
- **Action:** Clarify the outcome, classify regulatory urgency, assess architecture and testing impacts, quantify schedule and cost effects, identify displaced work, and route the change through agreed authority. Record the decision and conditions.
- **Result:** Scope changes are deliberate, traceable, and aligned to delivery priorities.
### SAP Payroll Example
Assess whether the change affects payroll schemas/rules, interfaces, country testing, employee communications, or reconciliation.
### SME Probe
What would justify bypassing the normal change process, and what evidence must still be retained?

## Q05 — How do you report programme status honestly?
### Interview Question
The steering committee expects a green status, but several critical acceptance criteria remain unproven. How do you report it?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Schedule optimism was pressuring teams to overstate readiness.
- **Task:** Give decision-makers an accurate view of delivery confidence.
- **Action:** Separate completed activity from accepted outcomes, show evidence against exit criteria, expose unresolved critical risks and dependencies, state forecast confidence, and present options with consequences. Escalate any request to conceal material risk.
- **Result:** Governance decisions are based on evidence rather than optimistic status labels.
### SAP Payroll Example
Report test pass rates alongside critical defects, unresolved reconciliation differences, payroll input completeness, and cutover readiness.
### SME Probe
How do you communicate uncertainty without making every workstream appear equally at risk?

## Q06 — How do you resolve cross-functional conflict?
### Interview Question
Payroll operations prioritises accuracy, while the programme prioritises date and finance prioritises close deadlines. How do you align them?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Workstream priorities conflicted and no shared decision criterion existed.
- **Task:** Reach a decision that protects business-critical outcomes.
- **Action:** Establish common objectives and non-negotiable controls, make trade-offs explicit, compare options by impact and reversibility, identify authorised decision-makers, and record the rationale and follow-up actions.
- **Result:** Conflict is converted into a transparent decision with clear accountability.
### SAP Payroll Example
Balance rollout timing with statutory deadlines, employee payment accuracy, finance posting, and support readiness.
### SME Probe
What do you do when there is no option that meets every stakeholder's preferred outcome?

## Q07 — How do you manage a vendor that is behind schedule?
### Interview Question
A payroll vendor repeatedly misses interface deliverables but reports progress as on track. What actions do you take?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Vendor status did not match the evidence required by dependent workstreams.
- **Task:** Restore delivery transparency and protect programme milestones.
- **Action:** Define objective acceptance evidence, review the deliverable and dependency chain, agree a dated recovery plan, track blockers and decision owners, and use contractual escalation where appropriate. Reassess downstream testing and cutover confidence.
- **Result:** Vendor performance is managed through verifiable outcomes and accountable recovery actions.
### SAP Payroll Example
Require tested interface contracts, error handling, reconciliation evidence, and operational documentation—not only a completed build.
### SME Probe
How do you distinguish vendor underperformance from an unresolved client-side dependency?

## Q08 — How do you manage resource contention?
### Interview Question
The same payroll SMEs are needed for design, testing, and business-as-usual processing. How do you plan capacity?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Key specialists were overcommitted across programme and operational responsibilities.
- **Task:** Protect critical knowledge and avoid hidden schedule assumptions.
- **Action:** Map demand by role and time period, identify peak payroll and statutory windows, agree capacity commitments with line managers, sequence work to reduce contention, and arrange deputies or targeted external support where appropriate.
- **Result:** The plan reflects realistic capacity and protects business-as-usual obligations.
### SAP Payroll Example
Reserve payroll SMEs for parallel runs, statutory validation, reconciliation, and cutover decisions during known critical periods.
### SME Probe
What is your contingency when the only subject-matter expert becomes unavailable?

## Q09 — How do you manage a major programme issue?
### Interview Question
A failed payroll rehearsal reveals a defect that could delay go-live. How do you lead the response?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** A rehearsal exposed a material issue with potential employee or compliance impact.
- **Task:** Stabilise the situation and produce an evidence-based recovery decision.
- **Action:** Confirm scope and severity, establish an incident lead, protect evidence, identify affected populations, contain further impact, assign root-cause analysis and remediation, and define retest and escalation criteria. Keep sponsors informed with options and consequences.
- **Result:** The issue is managed transparently with a controlled path to recovery or deferral.
### SAP Payroll Example
Trace the defect across input data, configuration, integration, calculation, posting, and payment handoff as relevant.
### SME Probe
What criteria determine whether the defect is a release blocker?

## Q10 — How do you plan steering committee decisions?
### Interview Question
Steering meetings review dozens of slides but decisions remain unresolved. How would you change the approach?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Governance time was consumed by status reporting without clear decision requests.
- **Task:** Make leadership forums outcome-oriented.
- **Action:** Circulate concise decision papers with context, options, evidence, recommendation, risk, cost/schedule impact, and decision deadline. Separate decisions from information items and record owners, rationale, and follow-up.
- **Result:** Governance time is used to unblock delivery and resolve material trade-offs.
### SAP Payroll Example
Bring clear decisions on cutover readiness, risk acceptance, country exceptions, provider dependencies, and release scope.
### SME Probe
What should happen when a decision owner misses the deadline and the dependency remains blocked?

## Q11 — How do you manage assumptions that become invalid?
### Interview Question
A plan assumes a provider can process a new country, but this is later challenged. What do you do?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** A foundational planning assumption lacked confirmed evidence.
- **Task:** Reassess delivery exposure and prevent the assumption from propagating.
- **Action:** Trace affected milestones and design choices, validate the assumption with authoritative evidence, quantify options and impact, update plans and RAID, and obtain decisions on scope, provider, or timing.
- **Result:** The programme adapts before an unverified assumption becomes a production dependency.
### SAP Payroll Example
Validate country coverage, localisation, provider responsibilities, data transfer, and statutory reporting before locking the rollout plan.
### SME Probe
How do you distinguish an assumption from a risk or an issue in programme governance?

## Q12 — How do you coordinate testing across workstreams?
### Interview Question
Payroll testing, integration testing, finance reconciliation, and user acceptance testing have separate calendars. How do you integrate them?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Workstream test plans missed cross-system scenarios and shared environment constraints.
- **Task:** Create an integrated assurance plan aligned to business processes.
- **Action:** Map end-to-end scenarios, data dependencies, environment readiness, test data, entry/exit criteria, defect triage, and retest windows. Coordinate ownership across workstreams and report coverage and unresolved risks.
- **Result:** Testing validates the payroll service end to end, not merely each component in isolation.
### SAP Payroll Example
Coordinate employee/time input, payroll calculation, posting, payment, statutory outputs, and downstream acknowledgements.
### SME Probe
How do you demonstrate coverage for a scenario that spans several independently owned systems?

## Q13 — How do you manage distributed stakeholders and country teams?
### Interview Question
Country teams have different calendars, languages, and local obligations. How do you maintain programme alignment?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Central communications were not sufficient for local teams to interpret and act on programme decisions.
- **Task:** Ensure shared standards are understood while local obligations remain visible.
- **Action:** Establish a stakeholder map, country leads, communication cadence, decision and escalation paths, local validation checkpoints, and clear documentation. Provide targeted briefings and track decisions and actions across time zones.
- **Result:** Country participation becomes structured and local risks surface early.
### SAP Payroll Example
Use country readiness reviews covering local payroll calendars, statutory requirements, providers, test evidence, and operational handover.
### SME Probe
How do you handle a local requirement that conflicts with a global milestone?

## Q14 — How do you manage programme benefits and costs?
### Interview Question
The programme is on schedule but forecast costs are rising and benefits are unclear. What do you do?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Delivery reporting focused on milestones while cost and benefit assumptions changed.
- **Task:** Restore visibility of financial performance and value realisation.
- **Action:** Reforecast remaining cost, explain variance drivers, reassess scope and risk, validate benefit assumptions with owners, and distinguish committed savings from capacity or risk benefits. Present options and update the governance baseline when approved.
- **Result:** Sponsors can make informed trade-offs based on forecast and expected outcomes.
### SAP Payroll Example
Include data remediation, parallel runs, integration build, localisation, provider transition, training, and hypercare in cost and benefit analysis.
### SME Probe
How do you prevent double-counting benefits across HR, finance, and shared services?

## Q15 — How do you manage delivery quality gates?
### Interview Question
Teams want to waive a readiness gate because most deliverables are complete. What is your response?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Schedule pressure encouraged treating completion percentages as a substitute for acceptance evidence.
- **Task:** Preserve meaningful quality and operational readiness controls.
- **Action:** Define gate criteria and authorised approvers in advance, assess each criterion against evidence, classify gaps by severity, document permissible exceptions, and block progression where non-negotiable controls are unmet.
- **Result:** Quality gates remain objective and exceptions have explicit ownership and consequences.
### SAP Payroll Example
Gate on reconciliation, critical defect status, input readiness, security, operational procedures, and payroll continuity arrangements.
### SME Probe
Which criteria should never be waived merely to preserve the date?

## Q16 — How do you manage cutover decision-making?
### Interview Question
The cutover plan is behind schedule, and the team proposes skipping a reconciliation checkpoint. How do you decide?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** A compressed timeline threatened a control designed to detect migration or processing errors.
- **Task:** Protect critical controls while assessing viable schedule options.
- **Action:** Determine the checkpoint's purpose and risk coverage, evaluate safe sequencing or recovery options, quantify the impact of deferral, and escalate to authorised decision-makers. Do not represent an omitted control as completed; record any approved alternative evidence.
- **Result:** Cutover decisions preserve transparency and minimum control requirements.
### SAP Payroll Example
Maintain signed data reconciliation and payroll validation evidence before switching production processing or payment responsibility.
### SME Probe
What alternative control could be acceptable, and who must approve it?

## Q17 — How do you manage post-go-live hypercare?
### Interview Question
Incident volumes spike after go-live, and project and support teams disagree about ownership. What do you do?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** The service experienced elevated issues during the transition to steady state.
- **Task:** Stabilise operations and ensure issues have clear owners.
- **Action:** Define hypercare command structure, severity and triage rules, daily metrics, escalation, vendor involvement, business impact assessment, and knowledge transfer. Track repeat incidents and exit criteria rather than ending hypercare solely on a fixed date.
- **Result:** Stabilisation is controlled and transition occurs when agreed service evidence supports it.
### SAP Payroll Example
Track payroll-critical interface failures, calculation defects, reconciliation breaks, payment issues, and employee support demand.
### SME Probe
What evidence is required to exit hypercare when incident volume is low but a critical defect remains?

## Q18 — How do you manage programme communications during a payroll incident?
### Interview Question
A payroll incident could delay employee payments, but the impact is not yet confirmed. What do you communicate?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Stakeholders needed timely information while the affected population and recovery time were still uncertain.
- **Task:** Communicate accurately without speculation or unnecessary disclosure.
- **Action:** State verified facts, known unknowns, affected processes, immediate containment, next update time, decision owner, and escalation route. Coordinate employee and leadership communications with authorised communications, HR, legal, and operations teams.
- **Result:** Stakeholders receive timely, consistent updates and understand what action is required.
### SAP Payroll Example
Distinguish a delayed interface from a confirmed payroll calculation or payment failure; do not imply employee impact until assessed.
### SME Probe
How do you correct an earlier update when new evidence changes the impact assessment?

## Q19 — How do you preserve delivery knowledge and decisions?
### Interview Question
A key architect leaves during implementation, and new team members cannot explain why important payroll design decisions were made. How do you prevent recurrence?
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** Critical design rationale existed mainly in individuals' knowledge.
- **Task:** Make decisions, assumptions, and operational knowledge transferable.
- **Action:** Maintain decision records, architecture views, interface catalogue, requirements traceability, risk history, test evidence, runbooks, and handover notes. Assign owners and review artefact completeness at phase gates.
- **Result:** The programme retains explainable decisions and reduces dependence on individual memory.
### SAP Payroll Example
Document payroll design choices, country deviations, reconciliation rules, interface recovery, and operational acceptance decisions.
### SME Probe
Which artefacts must be maintained as living records after the project closes?

## Q20 — How do you demonstrate delivery leadership under pressure?
### Interview Question
Describe how you would answer an interview question about recovering a payroll programme that is materially behind schedule.
### STAR Answer
- **Situation:** The programme had schedule slippage, unresolved dependencies, and reduced stakeholder confidence.
- **Task:** Establish a credible recovery plan while protecting employee, statutory, and financial controls.
- **Action:** Validate the true status, identify critical-path causes, re-sequence work, assign accountable owners, time-box decisions, make scope options explicit, and report progress against evidence. Protect non-negotiable controls and communicate trade-offs directly.
- **Result:** The programme has a realistic recovery baseline and transparent governance. In an interview, quantify the outcome only when supported by actual evidence.
### SAP Payroll Example
Use a real, appropriately anonymised example involving payroll inputs, integrations, parallel runs, cutover, or operational readiness.
### SME Probe
What would you do if the recovery plan still cannot meet the committed date without accepting unacceptable risk?
