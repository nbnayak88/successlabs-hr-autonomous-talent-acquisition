## Q01 — AI for Compensation Decision Support

### Interview Question
Where could AI add value in Compensation without replacing manager accountability?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers had difficulty interpreting large amounts of compensation and workforce information.  
**Task:** I needed to identify an AI use case that improved decisions without delegating reward authority to AI.  
**Action:** I identified bounded decision-support opportunities such as summarizing relevant data, surfacing anomalies, explaining trends, and suggesting questions for managers, while keeping final decisions human-owned.  
**Result:** AI became an augmentation layer rather than an uncontrolled decision-maker.

### SAP SuccessFactors Compensation & Variable Pay Example
Explore approved SAP Business AI/Joule capabilities where available for HR decision support, while keeping compensation decisions subject to enterprise governance.

### SME Probe
What Compensation decisions should remain explicitly human-controlled?

## Q19 — Innovation That Fails

### Interview Question
What would you do if an AI Compensation experiment fails to produce measurable value?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** An AI pilot produced technically interesting results but no meaningful business improvement.  
**Task:** I needed to avoid scaling technology simply because the experiment was innovative.  
**Action:** I reviewed the hypothesis, data quality, user adoption, workflow integration, success criteria, and actual business impact, then either redesigned the experiment or stopped it.  
**Result:** The organization learned from the experiment without creating unnecessary technology debt.

### SAP SuccessFactors Compensation & Variable Pay Example
Stop or redesign an AI insight pilot if managers do not use it, accuracy is inadequate, or the intervention does not improve reward decisions.

### SME Probe
