### HR-AGL4-B19-Q01 — Executive Business Case

**Interview Question:** A prospect wants to modernize succession planning but cannot justify the investment. How would you build the business case?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The prospect viewed Succession & Development as an HR technology expense.

**Task:** I needed to establish a business case tied to enterprise outcomes.

**Action:** I quantified critical-role exposure, readiness gaps, leadership continuity risk, manual effort, data fragmentation and development opportunities. I connected the target capabilities to measurable workforce outcomes and phased investment.

**Result:** The proposal became an enterprise workforce capability investment rather than a software purchase.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** I would position critical positions, successor coverage, readiness, talent profiles and development as measurable transformation capabilities.

**SME Probe:** Which assumptions in an HR technology business case should be validated first?

### HR-AGL4-B19-Q02 — Competing Solution Options

**Interview Question:** A client is considering three different succession platforms. How would you lead the decision?

**Situation:** The client had multiple viable platform options with different strengths.

**Task:** I needed to avoid turning the decision into a feature comparison.

**Action:** I established business and architecture criteria covering capability fit, process alignment, data, integration, security, experience, implementation complexity, total cost and strategic roadmap.

**Result:** Leadership could make a transparent decision based on enterprise fit rather than feature volume.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** SuccessFactors would be evaluated against the client's workforce strategy and target architecture, not simply against a checklist.

**SME Probe:** What criteria should carry the highest weight in a platform decision?

### HR-AGL4-B19-Q04 — Avoiding Over-Promise

**Interview Question:** A prospect asks whether SuccessFactors can solve every succession challenge. How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The prospect expected technology to solve organizational and workforce problems automatically.

**Task:** I needed to build trust without overpromising.

**Action:** I separated technology-enabled capabilities from operating-model, governance, talent-strategy and change requirements. I clearly identified where SuccessFactors could help and where organizational action remained necessary.

**Result:** The prospect received a credible transformation proposition instead of an unrealistic product promise.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** SuccessFactors can enable succession, talent profiles and development processes, but business governance and leadership accountability remain essential.

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A time-sensitive decision had significant long-term architecture implications.

**Task:** I needed to enable a fast decision without sacrificing critical analysis.

**Action:** I identified non-negotiable criteria, eliminated clearly unsuitable options, summarized remaining trade-offs and provided a recommendation with explicit risks and assumptions.

**Result:** Leadership could make a timely decision with controlled uncertainty.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** I would prioritize security, data, integration, standard capability, maintainability and business continuity over secondary feature differences.

### HR-AGL4-B19-Q06 — Leadership Through Ambiguity

**Interview Question:** Your transformation team has incomplete information and conflicting assumptions. How would you lead?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The team lacked reliable information about parts of the current succession landscape.

**Task:** I needed to create forward movement without pretending uncertainty did not exist.

**Action:** I separated facts, assumptions, decisions and unknowns; assigned owners to close critical gaps; and established decision checkpoints.

**Result:** The team could progress while maintaining visibility of uncertainty and risk.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** Unknowns around talent data, positions, readiness and integrations would become explicit architecture dependencies.

### HR-AGL4-B19-Q07 — Prioritizing Transformation Investments

**Interview Question:** You have funding for only three Succession & Development capabilities. How would you prioritize them?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Funding constraints required difficult prioritization.

**Task:** I needed to maximize business value rather than select the most attractive features.

**Action:** I ranked capabilities by business criticality, risk reduction, dependency, adoption readiness and measurable outcome. I protected foundational capabilities required for later phases.

**Result:** Investment was concentrated on capabilities with the highest enterprise value and strategic leverage.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** Critical-role visibility, reliable talent data and readiness/development capability could form a strong foundation.

**SME Probe:** Why should architecture dependencies influence prioritization?

**Interview Question:** Two executives disagree about the target succession strategy. How would you lead the decision?

**Task:** I needed to move the conversation from opinion to enterprise decision criteria.

**Action:** I compared capability gaps, time-to-readiness, business risk, internal mobility, external market constraints and development investment.

**Result:** Leadership could evaluate build-versus-buy choices using evidence and business context.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** Succession data could identify internal readiness while talent strategy and workforce planning could determine external requirements.

**SME Probe:** When should succession planning trigger an external talent strategy?

### HR-AGL4-B19-Q10 — Making a Go/No-Go Decision

**Interview Question:** The solution is technically ready but business adoption is weak. Would you recommend go-live?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Technical readiness was high, but managers had not demonstrated sufficient readiness to use the succession process.

**Task:** I needed to protect business outcomes rather than treat technical completion as go-live readiness.

**Action:** I assessed adoption risk, critical user journeys, training, communications, business ownership and support readiness. I recommended go-live only if risks were within agreed tolerance.

**Result:** The release decision reflected business readiness, not just technical readiness.

**SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example:** Manager completion of critical-role and successor activities would be part of readiness evidence.

**SME Probe:** What conditions justify delaying a technically ready release?
