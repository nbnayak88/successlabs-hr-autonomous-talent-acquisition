## HR-AGL4-B03-Q01 — Linking Succession to Business Continuity

### Interview Question
A business leader says succession planning is an HR exercise and not a business priority. How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization had succession records but leaders did not see a direct connection to business continuity.

**Task:** I needed to reposition succession as a business capability.

**Action:** I connected critical roles to operational dependency, leadership continuity, capability risk, and business impact. I then mapped successor readiness to the risk of an uncovered critical role.

**Result:** Leadership discussions shifted from maintaining HR records to protecting business continuity.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
I would use Succession & Development to identify critical positions, successors, readiness, and talent gaps that could affect continuity.

### SME Probe
How would you quantify the business impact of an uncovered critical role?

## HR-AGL4-B03-Q02 — Identifying Critical Roles

### Interview Question
A company wants to nominate hundreds of positions as critical. How would you establish a disciplined process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Every senior position was being labelled critical, making the designation meaningless.

**Task:** I needed to create a business-based critical-role process.

**Action:** I defined criteria such as strategic impact, scarcity of capability, replacement difficulty, operational dependency, regulatory exposure, and time-to-competence. I then introduced governance for periodic review.

**Result:** Critical-role status became evidence-based and useful for succession prioritization.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
Critical positions can be represented in succession planning so talent coverage can be assessed against the roles that matter most to the business.

### SME Probe
What criteria would make a role critical even when it is not an executive position?

## HR-AGL4-B03-Q03 — Succession Process from Identification to Readiness

### Interview Question
Describe the end-to-end succession process you would design for a global enterprise.

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization had isolated successor nominations but no consistent lifecycle.

**Task:** I needed to establish an enterprise succession process.

**Action:** I designed the flow: identify critical roles → define role requirements → assess talent → nominate successors → assess readiness → identify gaps → create development actions → review progress → refresh succession risk.

**Result:** Succession became a repeatable management process rather than an annual spreadsheet exercise.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
SuccessFactors can support critical positions, successor nominations, readiness information, talent profiles, and development planning across this lifecycle.

### SME Probe
Where should the process trigger intervention rather than merely record information?

## HR-AGL4-B03-Q04 — Connecting Potential and Performance

### Interview Question
A leadership team wants to promote the highest-performing employees automatically into succession pipelines. What would you do?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance was being treated as the only indicator of future leadership capability.

**Task:** I needed to protect the succession process from an overly narrow talent model.

**Action:** I separated current performance from future potential, role fit, leadership capability, experience, career aspiration, and development readiness. I used performance as one input rather than an automatic promotion rule.

**Result:** Succession decisions became more balanced and aligned to future-role requirements.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
Succession and talent review can use performance-related information alongside potential and other talent indicators.

### SME Probe
Why can a high performer still be a poor successor for a specific role?

## HR-AGL4-B03-Q09 — Career Aspirations in Succession

### Interview Question

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers nominated employees based on business need without validating career aspirations.

**Task:** I needed to align organizational succession needs with employee career intent.

**Action:** I introduced career-aspiration discussions and distinguished employee interest from manager nomination. Where aspirations did not align, I considered alternative successors or mobility paths.

**Result:** Succession became more employee-aware and reduced the risk of developing people for roles they did not want.

**Action:** I mapped future workforce capabilities to critical roles, assessed internal successor coverage, identified gaps, and fed those gaps into development, mobility, or external talent strategies.

**Result:** Succession became connected to future workforce requirements rather than focused only on current vacancies.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
