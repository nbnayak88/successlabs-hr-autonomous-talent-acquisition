## HR-AGL4-B01-Q01 — Succession as a Business Capability

### Interview Question
How would you explain succession planning as a business capability rather than an HR administrative process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization treated succession planning as an annual HR exercise focused mainly on maintaining successor lists.

**Task:** I needed to reposition succession as a capability for protecting critical business roles and workforce continuity.

**Action:** I connected critical roles, successor readiness, talent visibility, development actions, risk, workforce capability and business continuity. I used the talent process to identify where the organization had single-person dependencies and where development investment was required.

**Result:** Leadership could view succession as a business-risk and workforce-capability discipline rather than a yearly HR activity.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
I would use SuccessFactors Succession & Development to support successor identification, talent visibility, readiness assessment and development planning while keeping the business capability and decision framework outside the product boundary.

### SME Probe
What makes succession planning an enterprise capability rather than simply a feature of an HR system?

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q02 — Critical Role Identification

### Interview Question
How would you determine which roles should be treated as critical for succession planning?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization wanted to create a succession program but initially considered every position equally important.

**Task:** I needed to establish an evidence-based definition of role criticality.

**Action:** I evaluated business impact, scarcity of capability, replacement difficulty, regulatory exposure, revenue or operational dependency, strategic importance and continuity risk. I worked with business leaders to validate the resulting critical-role inventory.

**Result:** Succession investment became focused on roles where leadership continuity mattered most.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
I would use defined critical-role criteria to prioritize succession planning rather than treating the application as the source of the business definition.

### SME Probe
Would a highly paid role automatically be a critical role? Why or why not?

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q03 — Successor Readiness

### Interview Question
How would you define and assess successor readiness?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leaders had successor names but could not consistently explain how ready each person was.

**Task:** I needed to create a common readiness approach.

**Action:** I defined readiness criteria using capability, experience, performance evidence where appropriate, development progress, role-specific requirements, mobility constraints and estimated time to readiness. I separated evidence from subjective preference.

**Result:** Succession discussions became more consistent and actionable.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
SuccessFactors can provide structured talent and succession information, while the organization must define the business criteria and governance for readiness decisions.

### SME Probe
Why is a successor name without a readiness assessment insufficient?

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q04 — Talent Pool Design

### Interview Question
How would you design talent pools for an enterprise succession strategy?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization maintained informal lists of “high-potential” employees that varied by manager.

**Task:** I needed to create a more transparent and governed talent-pool approach.

**Action:** I defined pool purpose, eligibility criteria, evidence, governance, review cadence, development expectations and movement rules. I distinguished pools used for specific capability needs from broad labels of employee potential.

**Result:** Talent pools became actionable workforce-capability mechanisms rather than informal labels.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
SuccessFactors Succession & Development can support structured talent pools and talent visibility when business definitions and governance are clearly established.

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q05 — Potential vs Performance

### Interview Question
How would you explain the difference between employee performance and potential in succession planning?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers were using strong current performance as the primary reason for selecting successors.

**Task:** I needed to prevent performance and future potential from being treated as the same concept.

**Action:** I separated evidence of current-role performance from indicators of capacity for broader or different responsibilities. I established governance for potential assessment and required evidence rather than relying only on manager perception.

**Result:** Succession decisions became more disciplined and reduced the risk of promoting strong performers without assessing future-role fit.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
SuccessFactors can bring talent information together, but the organization must define how performance evidence and potential assessment are governed.

### SME Probe
Can a high performer have low succession potential for a specific role?

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q07 — Succession Risk

### Interview Question
How would you identify and communicate succession risk to executives?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Several critical roles had no credible ready successor.

**Task:** I needed to convert this gap into an actionable business-risk discussion.

**Action:** I assessed criticality, successor coverage, readiness, time to readiness, capability scarcity and business impact. I presented the risk together with mitigation options such as development, internal mobility, external talent strategy and contingency planning.

**Result:** Executives could prioritize succession actions according to business risk rather than simply reviewing talent lists.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
Succession information can provide visibility into successor coverage and readiness, while risk thresholds and mitigation decisions remain part of the business governance model.

### SME Probe
How would you distinguish a succession risk from a normal talent-development gap?

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q08 — Development as a Succession Mechanism

### Interview Question
How should development planning connect to succession planning?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization identified successors but development plans were maintained separately.

**Task:** I needed to connect succession gaps to development actions.

**Action:** I compared target-role requirements with successor capabilities and identified development gaps. I translated those gaps into measurable development actions, experiences, mentoring, learning or assignments and reviewed progress against readiness.

**Result:** Development became an active mechanism for reducing succession risk.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
SuccessFactors Development Planning can support development activities associated with talent and career objectives, while succession governance determines which gaps matter most.

### SME Probe
What is the difference between a development plan and a succession readiness plan?

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q12 — Successor Selection Bias

### Interview Question
How would you address bias in successor selection?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Successor decisions appeared to favor employees already known to senior leaders.

**Task:** I needed to improve decision quality and fairness.

**Action:** I introduced evidence-based criteria, calibration, diverse talent slates, structured review questions and monitoring of selection patterns. I also challenged assumptions about visibility, mobility and leadership style.

**Result:** Succession decisions became more transparent and the organization broadened its view of available talent.

## HR-AGL4-B01-Q14 — Talent Data Confidentiality

### Interview Question
Why is confidentiality particularly important in succession and talent processes?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Talent information included sensitive assessments, potential indicators and succession discussions.

**Task:** I needed to protect employees while enabling legitimate talent decisions.

**Action:** I classified sensitive information, applied least-privilege access, separated appropriate audiences, established governance and auditability, and trained stakeholders on responsible use.

**Result:** Talent decisions could be supported without unnecessarily exposing sensitive employee information.

### SAP SuccessFactors Succession & Development Example
Role-based permissions and governed access are essential when handling sensitive succession and talent information.

### SME Probe
Who should be allowed to see an employee's succession status, and why?
