# ALM6 — Theme 19: Presales, Leadership & Decision Making
## Applied SAP SuccessFactors Learning | Interview Preparation

**Theme outcome:** Lead solution conversations, qualify opportunities, build credible proposals, facilitate decisions, and guide teams toward outcomes without overpromising product capabilities or business results.

**Answer pattern:** Question → Situation → Task → Action → Result → SME Probe  
**Candidate rule:** Adapt examples to your actual experience. Illustrative outcomes are not personal achievements unless supported by evidence.

---

## 01. A prospect asks for a Learning demo before discovery

**Question:** A prospect wants a demo of SAP SuccessFactors Learning immediately, but you do not yet understand their business problem. How would you respond?

- **Situation:** The prospect is interested in a product demonstration but has not clarified the audience, pain points, or desired outcomes.
- **Task:** Build confidence while ensuring the demo addresses a real business need.
- **Action:** Acknowledge the request; ask a small set of discovery questions about learner groups, current process, compliance needs, content, integrations, and success measures; agree on one or two priority scenarios; demonstrate only relevant, verified capabilities; capture open questions and follow-up actions.
- **Result:** The demo becomes an evidence-based conversation about fit and outcomes rather than a generic feature tour.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Demonstrate a learner's assigned course journey, completion evidence, and manager reporting only where those capabilities are confirmed for the relevant configuration.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if the prospect insists on a full demo without sharing any context?

## 02. Qualify an opportunity with an unclear business case

**Question:** A prospect wants a Learning transformation but cannot explain the business value or secure executive sponsorship. Would you pursue the opportunity?

- **Situation:** There is interest, but the problem, decision process, and funding rationale are unclear.
- **Task:** Qualify the opportunity without dismissing a potentially valuable engagement.
- **Action:** Explore the problem, consequences of inaction, affected population, sponsor, decision criteria, budget path, timeline, and alternatives; identify the evidence required for a credible business case; propose a time-boxed discovery phase if appropriate; agree qualification milestones and a decision date.
- **Result:** The team can make a deliberate pursue, nurture, or decline decision based on evidence.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Explore whether the need is compliance evidence, content administration, assignment accuracy, learner experience, or integration reliability before proposing a Learning program.
- **SME probe:** Which missing qualification signals would make you stop investing presales effort?

## 03. A prospect requests an unsupported product capability

**Question:** During a presales call, the prospect asks for a Learning feature you cannot verify. How do you handle the question?

- **Situation:** A direct answer is expected, but product capability, release, licensing, or tenant configuration is uncertain.
- **Task:** Maintain credibility and prevent an unverified commitment.
- **Action:** Clarify the required behavior and acceptance criteria; state what is known and what needs confirmation; consult current official product documentation or the appropriate product specialist; record the question and owner; provide a time-bound follow-up; offer a clearly labeled alternative only if it is viable and validated.
- **Result:** The prospect receives a trustworthy answer and the proposal does not rely on a speculative feature.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Confirm the exact Learning behavior and applicable release or licensing before representing it as standard functionality.
- **SME probe:** How would you respond if a competitor claims that the capability is available out of the box?

## 04. Build a value proposition from a vague request

**Question:** A client says their current Learning platform is expensive and difficult to manage. How would you develop a value proposition?

- **Situation:** The client has described pain but has not quantified the impact or defined the desired future state.
- **Task:** Connect the proposed solution to measurable business outcomes.
- **Action:** Establish the current operating model and baseline; quantify administration effort, error rates, support demand, reporting delay, and learner friction where data exists; identify the highest-value use cases; map solution options to outcomes; state assumptions and costs; define a pilot and benefit-measurement approach.
- **Result:** The value proposition is traceable to client evidence and can be tested after implementation.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Build a case around verified improvements to assignment accuracy, compliance reporting, content operations, or learner access rather than generic claims of transformation.
- **SME probe:** How would you present value when the client has no reliable baseline data?

## 05. Compete against a lower-cost alternative

**Question:** A prospect says another provider can deliver the Learning solution for less. How would you defend your proposal?

- **Situation:** Price is a visible differentiator, but the scope, risk, and long-term operating model may not be comparable.
- **Task:** Enable a fair comparison without disparaging the competitor.
- **Action:** Confirm that both proposals cover the same business outcomes, populations, integrations, data migration, testing, adoption, support, and governance; compare total cost and risk over the relevant lifecycle; show evidence for differentiated strengths; identify scope choices that could reduce cost responsibly; allow the prospect to decide against agreed criteria.
- **Result:** The conversation moves from headline price to comparable value, scope, and risk.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Compare Learning implementation scope, content migration, integration assurance, compliance reporting, and post-go-live support across proposals.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if the lower-cost offer is genuinely the better fit?

## 06. Estimate effort when requirements are incomplete

**Question:** Sales leadership wants a fixed implementation estimate before requirements are fully understood. How would you respond?

- **Situation:** The estimate is being requested before complexity, dependencies, and scope are validated.
- **Task:** Provide useful planning guidance without creating false precision.
- **Action:** Separate known scope from assumptions and exclusions; identify major workstreams such as content, assignment design, integrations, reporting, security, testing, migration, and adoption; provide ranges or scenarios where appropriate; identify the discovery needed to narrow uncertainty; document change-control assumptions and estimate confidence.
- **Result:** Decision-makers can plan while understanding what could materially change the estimate.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Estimate based on learner populations, content inventory, integration count, reporting needs, and rollout regions only after clarifying the actual scope.
- **SME probe:** How would you handle a request to remove all contingency from the estimate?

## 07. The client expects guaranteed adoption or completion improvement

**Question:** A prospect asks you to guarantee a 20% improvement in mandatory learning completion. What would you say?

- **Situation:** The requested outcome depends on system design as well as policy, content, manager action, access, and learner behavior.
- **Task:** Set a credible target and define how the outcome will be evaluated.
- **Action:** Ask for the current baseline and population definition; identify controllable design factors and external dependencies; define leading measures such as assignment accuracy and successful access alongside completion outcomes; propose a pilot and measurement window; distinguish delivery commitments from business targets that require validation.
- **Result:** The proposal is ambitious but honest, with measurable criteria and explicit assumptions.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Commit to validated assignment and reporting behavior while treating a completion-rate uplift as a target to test against the agreed baseline.
- **SME probe:** How would you respond if the client makes the guarantee a contractual condition?

## 08. Lead a presales workshop with senior stakeholders

**Question:** You must facilitate a workshop with HR, Compliance, IT, procurement, and executive stakeholders. How would you ensure it produces a decision?

- **Situation:** Each group has different concerns and the meeting could become a sequence of disconnected feature requests.
- **Task:** Align participants on the problem, decision criteria, and next step.
- **Action:** Circulate a clear objective and pre-work; map stakeholder goals and decision rights; structure discussion around the learner journey and business outcomes; capture requirements, assumptions, risks, and trade-offs; time-box topics; summarize decisions and open questions; assign owners and deadlines.
- **Result:** The workshop produces an agreed problem statement, prioritized needs, and a path to a defensible solution decision.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Align stakeholders on mandatory learning populations, course lifecycle, completion evidence, integrations, reporting, and operating ownership.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if procurement focuses exclusively on cost while Compliance focuses exclusively on risk?

## 09. Decide whether to recommend a standard solution or customization

**Question:** A prospect asks for extensive customization. How would you decide what to recommend?

- **Situation:** Customization may address a stated need but can add delivery, support, security, and upgrade costs.
- **Task:** Recommend the simplest viable design that satisfies the real requirement.
- **Action:** Clarify the outcome and policy constraint; test standard capabilities and process redesign options; identify genuine gaps; compare alternatives across user value, total lifecycle cost, risk, maintainability, and upgrade impact; prototype uncertain high-value requirements; document the recommendation and its assumptions.
- **Result:** The proposal is tied to business need rather than customization volume.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Evaluate standard Learning assignments, curricula, notifications, and reporting before proposing external extensions.
- **SME probe:** What would justify accepting a customization that increases long-term maintenance?

## 10. Leadership disagrees on whether to pursue an opportunity

**Question:** Sales sees a strategic logo, but delivery believes the requirements and timeline are unrealistic. How would you facilitate a decision?

- **Situation:** Commercial value and delivery risk are being evaluated from different perspectives.
- **Task:** Make the decision based on transparent criteria and an agreed risk appetite.
- **Action:** Confirm the client's business need, decision process, scope, dependencies, and timeline; obtain delivery and product input; quantify the risk of commitments that cannot be validated; develop options such as phased scope, discovery first, revised dates, or no-bid; document assumptions and the accountable decision owner.
- **Result:** The organization makes an informed pursue/no-pursue decision and avoids hidden delivery commitments.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Assess whether a multi-region Learning rollout can meet the deadline given content readiness, data quality, integration dependencies, and testing capacity.
- **SME probe:** How would you challenge a senior sales leader's optimism without damaging collaboration?

## 11. Turn an architecture diagram into an executive decision

**Question:** You have prepared a detailed Learning architecture, but the steering committee is not engaging with the diagram. What would you change?

- **Situation:** The diagram may be technically accurate but does not make the business decision or trade-offs obvious.
- **Task:** Reframe the architecture around the committee's decision.
- **Action:** Start with the business outcome and current pain; show the main capabilities, systems, and data flows only as needed; highlight options, risks, dependencies, and ownership; move implementation details to an appendix; state the recommendation and decision requested; invite challenge on the assumptions.
- **Result:** The architecture becomes a decision-support tool rather than a static technical artifact.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Show how worker data leads to Learning assignments, how learners complete content, and how completion evidence reaches compliance reporting.
- **SME probe:** Which architecture details should remain visible to executives because they materially affect risk or cost?

## 12. A client wants a rapid proof of concept

**Question:** A prospect asks for a proof of concept (PoC) to validate a proposed Learning design. How would you scope it?

- **Situation:** The client wants evidence before making a larger investment, but an open-ended PoC could become an unpaid implementation.
- **Task:** Design a bounded experiment that resolves a specific uncertainty.
- **Action:** State the hypothesis and decision the PoC will inform; select a representative user journey; define test data, scope, exclusions, success criteria, duration, roles, and exit decision; use an appropriate non-production environment; document product or data limitations; conclude with evidence and a recommendation.
- **Result:** The PoC answers a decision-critical question and has a clear path to proceed, revise, or stop.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Test one representative assignment-to-completion-to-reporting flow rather than attempting to build the entire Learning solution.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if the client keeps adding features during the PoC?

## 13. An executive asks for a roadmap without a validated baseline

**Question:** Leadership wants a three-year Learning transformation roadmap, but current-state data is incomplete. How do you proceed?

- **Situation:** The organization wants strategic direction before its current capabilities and constraints are fully understood.
- **Task:** Create a useful roadmap while distinguishing evidence from hypotheses.
- **Action:** Define the target outcomes and principles; inventory known capabilities, pain points, risks, and dependencies; mark evidence confidence; establish discovery milestones to close critical gaps; sequence low-regret foundations before dependent investments; use decision gates to revisit priorities as evidence improves.
- **Result:** The roadmap guides action without pretending that every future decision is already certain.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Sequence governance and population/data validation before broad assignment automation or advanced learning analytics when those dependencies are unresolved.
- **SME probe:** How would you explain why a roadmap should evolve without making it appear that planning has failed?

## 14. A presales claim is challenged by a product specialist

**Question:** A colleague has promised a capability that the product specialist says is not confirmed. How would you handle the situation with the prospect?

- **Situation:** A prior statement may have created an expectation that cannot yet be supported.
- **Task:** Correct the record promptly and protect the credibility of the proposal.
- **Action:** Establish the exact statement and requirement; confirm the product facts with the specialist; inform the prospect transparently without blaming the colleague; explain what is verified, what is uncertain, and what alternatives exist; agree a written follow-up and update proposal assumptions.
- **Result:** The prospect can make a decision based on accurate capability information and the team avoids compounding an unverified promise.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Validate a Learning feature against current official documentation, release, licensing, and tenant configuration before reaffirming it.
- **SME probe:** How would you respond if correcting the claim could jeopardize the deal?

## 15. A transformation proposal ignores change management

**Question:** A proposal covers configuration and integrations but has little provision for adoption. How would you improve it?

- **Situation:** Technical delivery is defined, but stakeholder readiness and changed behaviors are not adequately addressed.
- **Task:** Ensure the solution can be adopted and operated, not merely deployed.
- **Action:** Identify impacted personas and changed tasks; assess manager, learner, administrator, and content-owner readiness; include communication, training, support, local champions, and feedback loops; define adoption measures; estimate effort and ownership; connect readiness gates to rollout milestones.
- **Result:** The proposal addresses the human and operating changes needed to realize business value.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Plan role-based preparation for learners, managers, Learning administrators, and content owners alongside configuration and testing.
- **SME probe:** How would you demonstrate the value of change management to a sponsor focused on implementation cost?

## 16. Delivery teams are not aligned with the presales promise

**Question:** After contract signature, the delivery team says the proposed scope is not feasible as written. What would you do?

- **Situation:** Sales commitments and delivery assumptions were not fully reconciled.
- **Task:** Resolve the gap transparently and establish a viable delivery baseline.
- **Action:** Compare the signed scope, assumptions, exclusions, and acceptance criteria with delivery's findings; identify what is feasible, uncertain, or impossible; assess contractual and business implications with the appropriate owners; propose options such as phased delivery, clarified scope, or a formal change; communicate one consistent position to the client.
- **Result:** The team establishes a realistic plan and handles the discrepancy through governance rather than informal promises.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Reconcile commitments about content migration, integrations, regional rollout, and reporting against actual inventories and dependencies.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if the contract language is ambiguous and each side interprets it differently?

## 17. Lead a decision under time and budget pressure

**Question:** A client must choose between a broader Learning scope with a longer timeline and a narrower release within budget. How would you advise them?

- **Situation:** The options optimize different goals and the client has limited capacity or funding.
- **Task:** Help the client select a viable path using explicit priorities and risk.
- **Action:** Confirm non-negotiable outcomes, deadlines, and controls; compare scope, value, dependencies, and residual risk; identify a minimum viable release and deferred capabilities; define how the later phases will be funded and governed; make the recommendation and decision criteria explicit.
- **Result:** The client chooses a staged or full-scope path with clear consequences and acceptance conditions.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Prioritize reliable mandatory assignments and completion evidence before lower-value dashboard enhancements if those are the agreed business priorities.
- **SME probe:** How would you prevent a minimum viable release from becoming a permanently incomplete solution?

## 18. Mentor a team member who dominates client discussions

**Question:** A technically strong consultant repeatedly dominates client meetings and misses stakeholder signals. How would you coach them?

- **Situation:** Expertise is valuable, but the interaction style prevents discovery and reduces client participation.
- **Task:** Improve consulting effectiveness while retaining technical strength.
- **Action:** Share specific observed examples and their impact; coach active listening and concise responses; introduce a question-first pattern; rehearse a discovery conversation; agree one behavior to practice in the next meeting; gather feedback and review progress.
- **Result:** The consultant learns to use expertise in service of the client's decision rather than overwhelming the conversation.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Coach the consultant to ask about learner, manager, and compliance outcomes before explaining Learning configuration options.
- **SME probe:** How would you coach them if the client explicitly asks for a deep technical explanation?

## 19. Measure presales effectiveness beyond revenue

**Question:** Leadership wants to know whether the presales architecture practice is effective. What measures would you propose?

- **Situation:** Revenue and win rate are important but do not show whether proposals are feasible, differentiated, or delivered successfully.
- **Task:** Create a balanced measurement approach.
- **Action:** Agree the practice's intended outcomes; measure qualification quality, decision-cycle time, proposal-to-delivery scope variance, assumption accuracy, PoC decision rate, client feedback, and delivery handover quality; define data sources and owners; segment by opportunity type; review trends and adjust the approach.
- **Result:** Leadership can assess both commercial performance and the quality and sustainability of solution commitments.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Track whether Learning proposals' assumptions about content, population, integration, and rollout are confirmed during delivery.
- **SME probe:** How would you avoid optimizing the metrics in ways that discourage pursuit of strategically valuable opportunities?

## 20. Establish a repeatable presales decision framework

**Question:** Your organization wants a common framework for evaluating Learning transformation opportunities. What would you design?

- **Situation:** Different teams use inconsistent criteria, producing uneven qualification and solution quality.
- **Task:** Create a lightweight, repeatable decision process without making every opportunity identical.
- **Action:** Define gates for problem clarity, strategic fit, stakeholder sponsorship, product fit, architecture feasibility, data and integration readiness, risk, value, delivery capacity, and commercial viability; specify evidence required at each gate; allow documented exceptions; assign decision owners; review outcomes and improve the framework.
- **Result:** Opportunities are evaluated consistently while preserving judgment for context-specific needs.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Require validated Learning use cases, population assumptions, content scope, integration dependencies, security controls, and success measures before finalizing a proposal.
- **SME probe:** How would you keep the framework adaptive as SAP capabilities and client needs change?

## Candidate self-review checklist

- [ ] Qualify the business problem before recommending a product.
- [ ] Verify product claims and state assumptions explicitly.
- [ ] Link the proposal to measurable value, scope, and risk.
- [ ] Present options and trade-offs, not just a preferred answer.
- [ ] Define PoC hypotheses and decision criteria.
- [ ] Reconcile presales commitments with delivery capability.
- [ ] Include adoption, support, and operating ownership.
- [ ] Make decisions traceable to evidence and accountable owners.

## Quality bar

A strong answer demonstrates **qualify → discover → shape → validate → quantify → decide → hand over**. Presales leadership is not about winning at any cost; it is about earning a viable commitment that can deliver the promised outcome.

**Boundary with adjacent themes:** Theme 18 focuses on communication and consulting conversations. Theme 19 applies those skills to opportunity qualification, proposal leadership, commercial trade-offs, and accountable decisions.
