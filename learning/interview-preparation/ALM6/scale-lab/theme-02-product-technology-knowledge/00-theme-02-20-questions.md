# ALM6 — Theme 02: Product / Technology Knowledge
## Applied SAP SuccessFactors Learning
**Theme:** Product / Technology Knowledge  
**Scenario range:** ALM6-T02-Q01–Q20  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate product-aware solution reasoning for SAP SuccessFactors Learning without reducing the interview to feature memorization.

Each scenario includes a situational question, STAR response, SAP SuccessFactors Learning example, and an SME probe. Adapt the results to evidence from your own projects; do not present illustrative metrics as personal achievements.

---

## Q01. Explain the core architecture of SAP SuccessFactors Learning.
**Question:** A global organization wants to replace disconnected LMS tools. How would you explain the core solution architecture and its boundaries?

**Situation:** Learning records, content delivery, and compliance reporting are spread across regional tools.  
**Task:** Explain how SAP SuccessFactors Learning fits into the wider HR technology landscape.  
**Action:** I would position Learning as the learning-management capability for items, curricula, programs, assignments, completions, and learning history. I would map identity and worker data dependencies, content delivery standards, reporting needs, and integrations with adjacent HR systems. I would distinguish native capabilities from external content providers, identity services, and analytics platforms.  
**Result:** Stakeholders receive a clear context diagram, system-of-record decisions, and an agreed boundary for the implementation.  
**SAP example:** SuccessFactors Learning manages learning assignments and completion history while employee attributes may be sourced from Employee Central and content may be delivered through supported content standards or external providers.  
**SME probe:** Which records must remain authoritative in Learning, and which should be mastered elsewhere?

## Q02. Choose between an item, curriculum, and program.
**Question:** Compliance training requires a single course, a multi-course learning path, and a scheduled cohort experience. How do you model these needs?

**Situation:** The business is using one generic object for different learning experiences.  
**Task:** Recommend the right learning constructs.  
**Action:** I would clarify the learner outcome, sequencing, recurrence, prerequisites, and scheduling requirements. I would use an item for a discrete learning activity, a curriculum to group required learning into a structured path, and a program where the business needs a coordinated learning experience with relevant scheduling or delivery characteristics. I would validate exact behavior against the tenant configuration and product release.  
**Result:** The design aligns learning objects to real learner and compliance needs rather than forcing every use case into one construct.  
**SAP example:** A safety course can be an item; a role-based safety pathway can be a curriculum; an instructor-led cohort experience may use a program and scheduled offering where supported by the design.  
**SME probe:** How would you handle a curriculum change when learners are already partway through it?

## Q03. Design a content strategy for multiple formats.
**Question:** A client needs SCORM courses, videos, virtual sessions, and external learning resources in one learning ecosystem. What is your approach?

**Situation:** Content teams have created inconsistent delivery methods and completion evidence.  
**Task:** Design a maintainable content model.  
**Action:** I would inventory formats, ownership, accessibility, versioning, completion rules, and learner experience. I would validate content compatibility and tracking requirements, define naming and metadata standards, and test representative content packages end to end. I would document which learning records are generated by the LMS versus an external provider.  
**Result:** Content is discoverable, trackable, and maintainable with fewer surprises during rollout.  
**SAP example:** Validate a SCORM package in the SuccessFactors Learning environment, then test launch, bookmarking, completion, score capture, and transcript updates against agreed requirements.  
**SME probe:** How do you distinguish a content-launch issue from a tracking or completion-rule issue?

## Q04. Explain assignment profiles and rule-based assignment.
**Question:** A company wants mandatory learning assigned automatically by job role, location, and worker population. How would you design it?

**Situation:** Administrators manually assign courses, leading to delays and missed learners.  
**Task:** Create controlled, scalable assignment logic.  
**Action:** I would identify the authoritative attributes, define audience rules and exceptions, map each rule to the relevant learning requirements, and test positive and negative cases. I would assess rule overlap, worker changes, effective timing, and reconciliation reporting. I would also define an owner for rule maintenance.  
**Result:** Assignments become repeatable and auditable, with a process for exceptions and rule changes.  
**SAP example:** Use supported assignment-profile capabilities to target a learning requirement by defined worker attributes, then validate assignment outcomes for transfers, new hires, and workers outside the target population.  
**SME probe:** What controls prevent conflicting rules from assigning inappropriate learning?

## Q05. Design prerequisites and completion criteria.
**Question:** Learners must complete foundational learning before advanced certification preparation. How would you enforce and verify the sequence?

**Situation:** Learners can start advanced content without demonstrating foundational knowledge.  
**Task:** Establish a defensible progression model.  
**Action:** I would define prerequisite evidence, acceptable completion states, exception handling, and whether the requirement is enforced by the learning object configuration or by an operational process. I would test completed, incomplete, waived, expired, and equivalency cases.  
**Result:** Learners follow the intended sequence and administrators can explain why a learner is or is not eligible.  
**SAP example:** Configure and test the relevant prerequisite and curriculum behavior in Learning, using test users with different transcript states.  
**SME probe:** How would you handle equivalent learning completed in a legacy LMS?

## Q06. Configure instructor-led training at scale.
**Question:** A global company needs classroom and virtual sessions with capacity limits, waitlists, attendance, and cancellations. How would you approach the design?

**Situation:** Regional teams manage sessions in spreadsheets and have inconsistent attendance records.  
**Task:** Standardize event-based learning operations.  
**Action:** I would map the learner journey from discovery and registration through scheduling, attendance, completion, cancellation, and follow-up. I would identify supported offering and session capabilities, roles, capacity rules, notifications, and regional variations. I would test full, cancelled, rescheduled, and no-show scenarios.  
**Result:** Session operations become consistent and learning history reflects actual participation.  
**SAP example:** Model instructor-led learning with the appropriate item and scheduled offering configuration, then validate registration and completion behavior in the tenant.  
**SME probe:** What is the operational impact of recording attendance late or against the wrong offering?

## Q07. Explain equivalencies and credit for prior learning.
**Question:** A learner completed an approved external course and should not repeat the same requirement. What design do you recommend?

**Situation:** Duplicate assignments create frustration and unnecessary cost.  
**Task:** Recognize valid prior learning without weakening compliance controls.  
**Action:** I would define approved evidence, equivalency criteria, approval authority, effective dates, and audit requirements. I would distinguish formal equivalency rules from manually recorded external completions and test how each affects the learner’s transcript and downstream reports.  
**Result:** Valid prior learning is recognized consistently while the organization retains evidence and governance.  
**SAP example:** Use supported equivalency or external-completion processes in SuccessFactors Learning, based on the client’s configured capabilities and control requirements.  
**SME probe:** How would you prevent an unverified external certificate from satisfying a regulated requirement?

## Q08. Plan learning item revisions and version control.
**Question:** A regulated course changes annually. How do you manage revisions without losing historical evidence?

**Situation:** The compliance team needs current content while auditors may ask what a learner completed in a prior year.  
**Task:** Design the content lifecycle and evidence model.  
**Action:** I would establish versioning conventions, review and approval steps, effective dates, change impact assessment, learner reassignment rules, and retention expectations. I would test the impact of changing an item on active assignments and historical records before production release.  
**Result:** Current requirements can be enforced while historical learning evidence remains interpretable.  
**SAP example:** Revise a controlled learning item and verify the tenant’s behavior for existing assignments, completions, and reporting before approving the change.  
**SME probe:** How do you decide whether a revision requires retraining or only content maintenance?

## Q09. Build a learning security and administrator-role model.
**Question:** Regional administrators should manage their own learning operations but must not access every worker’s records. How do you design access?

**Situation:** The current model grants broad administrator access to solve operational problems.  
**Task:** Apply least privilege while preserving supportability.  
**Action:** I would map personas to tasks, separate configuration and operational duties where feasible, define regional or population boundaries, and validate permissions with positive and negative tests. I would include joiner/mover/leaver controls, periodic access reviews, and an escalation path.  
**Result:** Administrators can perform required work without unnecessary access to sensitive learning records.  
**SAP example:** Review SuccessFactors Learning role and permission configuration with the client’s broader identity and authorization model; verify actual access using test accounts.  
**SME probe:** What evidence would you provide to demonstrate that segregation of duties is working?

## Q10. Design learning notifications and reminders.
**Question:** Learners miss deadlines because reminders are inconsistent across regions. What would you investigate and configure?

**Situation:** Compliance completion is delayed and managers cannot tell whether reminders were sent.  
**Task:** Create a reliable notification experience.  
**Action:** I would map triggering events, recipient rules, language, timing, escalation, and channel constraints. I would review supported notification configuration, avoid excessive reminder frequency, and test assignment, due-date, completion, cancellation, and exception scenarios. I would define monitoring for failed or misunderstood notifications.  
**Result:** Learners receive actionable messages and managers have a clearer escalation path.  
**SAP example:** Validate configured Learning notifications against test assignments and due dates, checking both the trigger and the recipient resolution.  
**SME probe:** How would you diagnose a reminder that was configured but not received?

## Q11. Integrate Learning with Employee Central.
**Question:** Worker changes in Employee Central should influence learning audiences and assignments. How would you design the integration?

**Situation:** Transfers and terminations leave learners in incorrect groups or with stale assignments.  
**Task:** Establish reliable worker-data dependencies.  
**Action:** I would identify required worker attributes, source-of-truth rules, event or batch timing, mapping, error handling, reconciliation, and privacy constraints. I would test hire, transfer, manager change, location change, leave, and termination scenarios, including delayed or failed updates.  
**Result:** Learning assignments and access reflect worker changes with measurable reconciliation and support procedures.  
**SAP example:** Validate the configured Employee Central-to-Learning data flow and assignment rules in the target tenant; do not assume every attribute or event behaves identically across configurations.  
**SME probe:** What is your recovery approach when worker data is updated but the learning assignment is not?

## Q12. Connect external content providers or libraries.
**Question:** The business wants to add an external learning library while keeping learning activity visible in its LMS. How do you assess the integration?

**Situation:** Content discovery is fragmented and completion data may not return to the LMS.  
**Task:** Select an integration pattern that preserves a coherent learner and reporting experience.  
**Action:** I would compare launch/link, supported content-package, and provider integration options based on authentication, launch behavior, tracking, completion evidence, licensing, and support ownership. I would verify product and provider prerequisites, then run end-to-end tests for launch, progress, completion, and error recovery.  
**Result:** The organization chooses a pattern based on verified tracking and operational needs, not only content availability.  
**SAP example:** Pilot one representative provider course and confirm what completion data is returned to SuccessFactors Learning before scaling the catalogue.  
**SME probe:** What should happen when a learner completes the external course but the LMS transcript remains unchanged?

## Q13. Explain learning history, transcript, and audit evidence.
**Question:** An auditor requests proof that a population completed mandatory training by a specific date. How would you produce defensible evidence?

**Situation:** Completion reports disagree with local spreadsheets.  
**Task:** Define the evidence source and reporting logic.  
**Action:** I would confirm the population, requirement, effective date, completion definition, status semantics, and required fields. I would extract or report from the authoritative learning records, reconcile exceptions, preserve report parameters, and document any manual corrections or external evidence.  
**Result:** The audit response is reproducible, traceable, and transparent about exceptions.  
**SAP example:** Use the tenant’s supported Learning reporting and transcript data to reconcile assigned, completed, overdue, and exempt populations.  
**SME probe:** How would you explain the difference between a course being launched and its completion being recorded?

## Q14. Troubleshoot failed content launch or tracking.
**Question:** Users can open a course, but their progress is not recorded. How do you isolate the issue?

**Situation:** Learners report completed courses still showing as incomplete.  
**Task:** Diagnose the failure without making uncontrolled production changes.  
**Action:** I would reproduce the issue with a controlled test user, capture the exact content package and browser/session behavior, check launch and tracking standards, inspect completion criteria and relevant configuration, and compare a known-good package. I would isolate content, browser, authentication, network, and LMS configuration causes, then retest after a controlled fix.  
**Result:** Root cause is evidenced and the resolution is validated across representative users and content.  
**SAP example:** Test SCORM launch, status communication, score/progress tracking, and completion criteria in a non-production environment before deploying a corrected package.  
**SME probe:** What evidence differentiates a package defect from a completion-rule configuration issue?

## Q15. Design learning analytics and operational KPIs.
**Question:** Executives want to know whether learning is improving capability, not just completion rates. What measures would you propose?

**Situation:** Existing dashboards report course counts but do not connect learning to business needs.  
**Task:** Build a layered measurement model.  
**Action:** I would separate operational measures (assignment, completion, overdue, failure), experience measures (accessibility, learner feedback, drop-off), capability measures (assessment or skill evidence), and business measures (time to proficiency, quality, safety, or service outcomes where causality can be assessed). I would define each metric, denominator, owner, refresh cadence, and limitations.  
**Result:** Leaders can distinguish activity from learning evidence and business impact.  
**SAP example:** Combine available Learning completion and assessment data with governed workforce or business measures in an approved analytics environment.  
**SME probe:** How would you avoid claiming that a course caused a business improvement when other factors changed?

## Q16. Plan learning data migration from a legacy LMS.
**Question:** The organization is moving five years of learning history into SuccessFactors Learning. How do you decide what to migrate?

**Situation:** Legacy records contain duplicates, inconsistent statuses, and incomplete evidence.  
**Task:** Protect required history while controlling migration risk.  
**Action:** I would profile source data, classify records by legal, audit, operational, and learner-value needs, map fields and statuses, define cleansing and exception rules, and agree on a migration cutoff. I would run mock loads, reconcile counts and samples, validate learner visibility and reports, and secure business sign-off.  
**Result:** The migration preserves agreed evidence and makes residual data limitations explicit.  
**SAP example:** Map legacy learner identifiers, learning item references, completion dates, statuses, and scores to the supported migration approach, then reconcile migrated transcripts in test cycles.  
**SME probe:** Which legacy records would you retain outside the new LMS rather than transform into misleading learning history?

## Q17. Support localization and accessibility.
**Question:** A learning experience must serve employees in multiple languages and accommodate accessibility needs. What should the solution architect address?

**Situation:** A global course works for headquarters but fails regional usability and accessibility checks.  
**Task:** Design inclusive learning delivery without uncontrolled duplication.  
**Action:** I would define language ownership, translation workflow, localized metadata, accessible content standards, captions, keyboard and screen-reader testing, regional delivery constraints, and content-version alignment. I would test representative languages and learner devices, and establish how localized versions remain synchronized with the approved source.  
**Result:** Learners receive a consistent and inclusive experience with governed local adaptation.  
**SAP example:** Validate localized item metadata and the associated content package in Learning, including launch, completion, and accessibility checks.  
**SME probe:** How do you keep translated content aligned when the source course changes?

## Q18. Manage learning environments and release changes.
**Question:** A Learning configuration change is planned shortly before a mandatory training campaign. How do you control release risk?

**Situation:** Prior changes caused unexpected assignment and reporting behavior.  
**Task:** Establish a safe change and deployment approach.  
**Action:** I would document the change, dependencies, risk, test cases, rollback or mitigation plan, and business owner. I would test in the appropriate non-production environment, validate integrations and representative learner journeys, schedule a controlled release window, and monitor agreed post-release indicators. I would confirm tenant-specific release and configuration constraints rather than assume identical behavior everywhere.  
**Result:** The release is evidence-based, traceable, and recoverable if an issue occurs.  
**SAP example:** Regression-test assignment, launch, completion, notifications, permissions, and reports after a Learning configuration change.  
**SME probe:** Which tests are mandatory before a change that affects compliance assignments?

## Q19. Diagnose performance and scale concerns.
**Question:** A global campaign causes slow access and a spike in support tickets. How would you determine whether the issue is platform, content, integration, or user experience related?

**Situation:** Complaints arrive from several regions during a deadline period.  
**Task:** Prioritize evidence collection and restore service safely.  
**Action:** I would establish scope and timeline, compare affected and unaffected users, capture error patterns and timings, inspect content size and launch behavior, check relevant integration and identity dependencies, and coordinate with platform/provider support where necessary. I would prioritize safe mitigations, communicate status, and follow up with a root-cause review.  
**Result:** The team narrows the fault domain quickly and avoids unsupported configuration changes during an incident.  
**SAP example:** Compare the same course launch and completion path across controlled test accounts and regions, then correlate findings with available tenant and provider diagnostics.  
**SME probe:** What signals would make you escalate to SAP or the content provider rather than change LMS configuration?

## Q20. Recommend a product capability based on business value.
**Question:** A sponsor asks for a new Learning feature because a competitor uses it. How do you decide whether to implement it?

**Situation:** The requested capability may add complexity without addressing the real learning problem.  
**Task:** Make a defensible product and architecture recommendation.  
**Action:** I would restate the target learner and business outcome, map the current pain point, compare standard configuration, process change, integration, and extension options, and assess usability, compliance, cost, maintainability, security, and adoption. I would validate capability and licensing in current product documentation and the target tenant, then propose a pilot with measurable acceptance criteria.  
**Result:** The decision is based on verified capability and outcome evidence rather than feature novelty.  
**SAP example:** Pilot a relevant native Learning capability with a representative learner group and compare completion friction, support demand, and required governance against the baseline.  
**SME probe:** What evidence would make you recommend not implementing a requested feature?
