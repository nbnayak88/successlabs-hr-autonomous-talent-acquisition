# ALM6 — Theme 17: Stakeholder Management
## Applied SAP SuccessFactors Learning | Interview Preparation

**Theme outcome:** Align business, HR, compliance, IT, learning-content owners, vendors, and learners around Learning decisions, trade-offs, ownership, and measurable outcomes.

**Answer pattern:** Question → Situation → Task → Action → Result → SME Probe  
**Candidate rule:** Adapt the examples to your actual experience. Illustrative outcomes are not personal achievements unless you can substantiate them.

---

## 01. Compliance and Learning teams disagree on the launch date

**Question:** Compliance insists that mandatory training launch this week, while the Learning team says the course is not ready. How would you facilitate a decision?

- **Situation:** Two accountable teams have competing priorities: deadline adherence and launch quality.
- **Task:** Establish a decision based on evidence, risk, and ownership rather than hierarchy alone.
- **Action:** Clarify the compliance deadline and consequence of delay; review content approval, assignment accuracy, accessibility, completion tracking, and reporting readiness; distinguish blockers from manageable defects; present launch, limited rollout, or delay options with residual risks; document the decision owner and acceptance criteria.
- **Result:** Stakeholders make a transparent, risk-based decision and understand the conditions required for launch.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Use end-to-end evidence from assignment through course launch, assessment, completion, and compliance reporting to support the go/no-go discussion.
- **SME probe:** How would you proceed if the deadline is regulatory but a critical completion-tracking defect remains?

## 02. Executives want a completion percentage without agreeing on the population

**Question:** Leaders request a single completion figure, but HR and Compliance disagree about which workers belong in the denominator. What do you do?

- **Situation:** A high-visibility metric has no shared population definition.
- **Task:** Align stakeholders on a defensible measure before presenting it as fact.
- **Action:** Identify the policy owner and reporting purpose; document the in-scope population, exclusions, effective date, due-date logic, and approved exceptions; reconcile the proposed definition against source populations; show the impact of alternative definitions; obtain sign-off and publish the definition alongside the metric.
- **Result:** Leadership receives a reproducible measure and can interpret changes without hidden denominator shifts.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Reconcile Learning assignments and completion data against the approved worker population and report filters.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if executives demand a number before the definition is approved?

## 03. Managers do not act on overdue-learning reports

**Question:** Managers receive overdue-learning reports but do not follow up with their teams. How would you improve engagement?

- **Situation:** Reporting is available, but the intended management action is not happening.
- **Task:** Understand the adoption barrier and establish accountability for follow-up.
- **Action:** Interview a sample of managers; test whether reports are timely, relevant, understandable, and actionable; clarify who owns reminders and escalation; agree a simple follow-up workflow and due dates; provide targeted guidance; measure acknowledgment and resolution rates rather than email volume alone.
- **Result:** The process connects reporting to named actions and gives leaders visibility into unresolved cases.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Review the manager's Learning reporting view, population scope, and available notification or escalation capabilities in the configured tenant.
- **SME probe:** How would you distinguish manager unwillingness from a report or process design problem?

## 04. A global template conflicts with a regional stakeholder's preference

**Question:** A regional HR leader requests a Learning configuration that differs from the global template. How do you handle the request?

- **Situation:** The request may reflect a genuine local obligation, a process need, or a preference.
- **Task:** Protect justified local needs without creating uncontrolled variation.
- **Action:** Ask for the business outcome and supporting requirement; distinguish legal or policy obligations from convenience; assess effects on learners, data, reporting, support, and upgrades; compare standard options; document the exception and its owner; route the decision through architecture and governance forums.
- **Result:** The decision is consistent with global principles while allowing approved local variation when justified.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Compare local item, curriculum, language, or reporting needs against the global Learning design and its support model.
- **SME probe:** What evidence would justify changing the global template instead of granting a local exception?

## 05. A vendor and internal team dispute responsibility for a defect

**Question:** A Learning vendor says the problem is caused by the customer configuration, while the internal team blames the vendor package. How would you resolve the dispute?

- **Situation:** Conflicting explanations are delaying resolution and eroding trust.
- **Task:** Establish a shared evidence base and clear ownership for next actions.
- **Action:** Agree on one reproducible example and expected behavior; capture the package version, configuration, timestamps, and observed result; assign each party a testable hypothesis; time-box experiments; record evidence and findings in a shared issue log; agree the corrective owner and retest criteria.
- **Result:** Resolution progresses through evidence rather than blame, and responsibilities become explicit.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Reproduce the same course-launch or completion-tracking issue with the relevant item version and tenant configuration.
- **SME probe:** How would you handle a defect with contributing causes across both vendor content and customer configuration?

## 06. Learners say the course is confusing, while the subject-matter expert says it is correct

**Question:** How would you reconcile learner feedback with the content owner's view that the course is technically accurate?

- **Situation:** Accuracy and usability are being treated as if they were the same quality dimension.
- **Task:** Preserve subject accuracy while making the learning experience effective.
- **Action:** Review the intended learning outcomes; observe representative learners attempting key tasks; categorize feedback into terminology, sequencing, accessibility, navigation, and factual errors; share evidence with the content owner; prototype improvements; validate accuracy and comprehension before release.
- **Result:** The discussion shifts from opinion to observable learner performance and approved content quality.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Use learner feedback and assessment patterns alongside item content and completion data to prioritize course improvements.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if a simplification improves comprehension but risks changing a technical meaning?

## 07. A senior sponsor asks for an unapproved manual completion update

**Question:** A senior leader wants a group marked complete before an audit, but the required evidence and approval are missing. How do you respond?

- **Situation:** The request is urgent and influential, but it could undermine record integrity.
- **Task:** Protect the control while offering a practical, compliant route to resolution.
- **Action:** Acknowledge the deadline; explain the audit and policy risk; identify acceptable evidence and the authorized approver; offer a rapid review through the approved correction or external-learning process; communicate the timing and unresolved cases; escalate only through the defined governance path if pressure continues.
- **Result:** The issue is handled respectfully without representing unsupported completion as fact.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Use the approved Learning completion-correction process and retain evidence rather than changing status informally.
- **SME probe:** How would you preserve the relationship if the sponsor views the control as bureaucracy?

## 08. IT prioritizes another project over a Learning integration defect

**Question:** A delayed integration is preventing timely Learning assignments, but the IT integration team has competing priorities. How would you gain commitment?

- **Situation:** The issue spans team boundaries and competes with other work.
- **Task:** Explain business impact and secure a proportionate, agreed response.
- **Action:** Quantify affected learners, delay duration, compliance or onboarding impact, and available workaround; distinguish immediate containment from permanent remediation; present options with effort and risk; agree an accountable owner, milestone, and escalation threshold; provide concise updates against the agreed plan.
- **Result:** Prioritization is based on business impact and a realistic delivery agreement rather than repeated escalation alone.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Show how the HR-to-Learning data delay affects the new-hire assignment journey and the associated compliance measure.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if IT still cannot commit within the required timeframe?

## 09. Security and learner experience teams disagree on access controls

**Question:** Security proposes stricter authentication that could make Learning harder for frontline employees. How would you facilitate the design?

- **Situation:** Security assurance and practical access are both legitimate requirements.
- **Task:** Find an approved solution that meets identity controls and frontline operating needs.
- **Action:** Map frontline device, network, shift, and identity constraints; define the threat and control objective with Security; assess supported access options; test representative scenarios, including shared-device privacy and individual completion attribution; compare risk and usability evidence; document the approved decision and exceptions.
- **Result:** The selected design makes trade-offs explicit and is validated with both security and learner representatives.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Test the actual Learning sign-in and launch journey using supported authentication and device configurations.
- **SME probe:** Which requirements are non-negotiable, and where can experience design provide flexibility?

## 10. Two business owners give contradictory requirements

**Question:** One owner wants a course assigned to all employees, while another says only designated roles should receive it. How would you proceed?

- **Situation:** Conflicting requirements could create unnecessary workload or leave a required population uncovered.
- **Task:** Resolve the conflict using policy, risk, and accountable decision rights.
- **Action:** Trace each request to its underlying business objective; identify the policy owner and the authoritative population rule; model the consequences of each option; validate sample records; obtain a documented decision from the appropriate authority; translate the decision into testable assignment criteria and regression cases.
- **Result:** The rule has a clear owner, rationale, and evidence-based test rather than an ambiguous compromise.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Test Learning assignment profile criteria against both in-scope and explicitly out-of-scope user records.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if neither requester has authority to decide the policy question?

## 11. A project is slipping because decisions are not being made

**Question:** Learning design decisions repeatedly remain open, causing downstream configuration and testing delays. How would you intervene?

- **Situation:** Delivery risk is growing because decision rights, deadlines, or required evidence are unclear.
- **Task:** Make decisions easier to take and visible to the right people.
- **Action:** Create a decision log with the question, options, recommendation, evidence, decision owner, deadline, and impact of delay; separate reversible choices from high-impact decisions; bring only unresolved trade-offs to the appropriate forum; time-box decisions where reasonable; escalate overdue decisions with their consequences and a proposed path.
- **Result:** Stakeholders understand what is needed, who must decide, and what delay costs the project.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Track unresolved decisions on curricula, assignment rules, content versions, and completion reporting that block Learning configuration or testing.
- **SME probe:** How would you handle a decision that is urgent but based on incomplete evidence?

## 12. Change communications are not reaching the intended audience

**Question:** A Learning rollout is technically ready, but employees and managers are unaware of what will change. What do you do?

- **Situation:** Deployment readiness does not guarantee stakeholder awareness or adoption.
- **Task:** Deliver role-specific communications that prepare people for the new journey.
- **Action:** Segment audiences by role, region, language, and impact; identify what each group must know or do; select approved communication channels and timing; explain the action, deadline, support route, and expected behavior; validate messages with local representatives; measure reach and readiness through meaningful checks.
- **Result:** Communications become actionable and readiness gaps can be addressed before launch.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Communicate new Learning assignment behavior, course access, completion expectations, and support routes to learners and managers.
- **SME probe:** How would you validate understanding rather than just message delivery?

## 13. A stakeholder wants a feature outside the agreed scope

**Question:** A business sponsor requests an additional Learning dashboard shortly before go-live. How do you manage the request?

- **Situation:** The request may add value, but could affect schedule, testing, and release stability.
- **Task:** Evaluate the request transparently without dismissing the business need.
- **Action:** Clarify the outcome and urgency; classify it as a go-live requirement, risk mitigation, or enhancement; estimate scope and dependencies; assess security, data definition, testing, and deployment impact; present options such as defer, phase, or trade scope; obtain the proper decision and update the plan.
- **Result:** The sponsor understands the trade-off and the team protects release readiness through an explicit decision.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Assess a proposed Learning dashboard against report-definition approval, access controls, and regression-testing needs.
- **SME probe:** What evidence would justify bringing the request into the current release?

## 14. A stakeholder rejects the recommended standard solution

**Question:** You recommend standard Learning functionality, but a business leader insists on a custom approach. How would you respond?

- **Situation:** The disagreement may reflect unmet needs, prior experience, or concerns that have not been made explicit.
- **Task:** Understand the objection and reach a defensible architecture decision.
- **Action:** Ask which outcome the standard option fails to deliver; demonstrate the standard journey using realistic scenarios; compare options across user value, control, supportability, upgrade impact, cost, and delivery risk; record assumptions and gaps; recommend a time-boxed prototype or governance decision if uncertainty remains.
- **Result:** The decision is grounded in evidence and long-term consequences rather than personal preference.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Compare standard assignment, curriculum, notification, and reporting options with the documented requirement before accepting customization.
- **SME probe:** How would you proceed if the standard option is supportable but materially less usable?

## 15. A regional team is resisting a global Learning rollout

**Question:** A regional team says the global solution does not fit its workflows and refuses to participate in testing. How would you engage them?

- **Situation:** Low regional participation may expose real design gaps or concerns about ownership and change.
- **Task:** Understand resistance, validate requirements, and secure meaningful participation.
- **Action:** Meet regional representatives to identify specific pain points; distinguish mandatory local needs from preferences; demonstrate the proposed journey; involve them in scenario-based testing; record defects and accepted differences; agree local champions, responsibilities, and readiness criteria; escalate unresolved policy conflicts through governance.
- **Result:** The region has a clear route to influence the design and a shared basis for readiness decisions.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Include regional users in tests for localized content, assignment rules, learner access, and reporting.
- **SME probe:** What would you do if the region continues to refuse participation after valid concerns are addressed?

## 16. The implementation partner reports green status, but users report serious problems

**Question:** The partner's project dashboard is green, but learners cannot reliably complete courses. How do you challenge the status constructively?

- **Situation:** Project status indicators may not reflect the actual end-to-end user outcome.
- **Task:** Establish a shared readiness view based on agreed evidence.
- **Action:** Ask for the exit criteria and supporting test evidence; reproduce representative learner journeys; compare defect severity, test coverage, and user feedback; agree a common definition of green status; document gaps and owners; report the business impact and decision required without personalizing the disagreement.
- **Result:** Readiness reporting reflects learner outcomes and explicit acceptance criteria rather than status labels alone.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Validate assignment, launch, assessment, completion, and reporting before accepting a Learning release as ready.
- **SME probe:** Which evidence should outweigh a high test pass percentage?

## 17. An incident update needs to reach executives and technical teams

**Question:** A major Learning incident is affecting mandatory training. How do you communicate to both executives and technical responders?

- **Situation:** Different audiences need different levels of detail, but all need a consistent account of impact and next steps.
- **Task:** Maintain trust and coordinate action without speculation.
- **Action:** Establish confirmed facts, affected population, business impact, current containment, unknowns, owner, and next update time; give executives concise impact and decision needs; give technical teams reproducible evidence and investigation tasks; maintain one incident timeline; correct misinformation promptly and close with verified recovery evidence.
- **Result:** Stakeholders receive consistent updates suited to their responsibilities and the response remains coordinated.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Communicate the impact of a Learning launch or completion-record incident separately from technical diagnostic detail.
- **SME probe:** How would you communicate when the cause is unknown and the next update may not contain a resolution?

## 18. A steering committee must choose between two rollout options

**Question:** The steering committee must choose between a single global launch and a phased regional launch. How would you support the decision?

- **Situation:** Each option has different trade-offs in speed, consistency, operational risk, and regional readiness.
- **Task:** Make the decision criteria and consequences clear.
- **Action:** Define success criteria; compare readiness, dependencies, support capacity, localization, data quality, and compliance deadlines; estimate the consequences of failure and recovery options; identify assumptions and confidence levels; recommend the option that best meets the agreed priorities; document decision rights, triggers, and review points.
- **Result:** The committee makes an informed choice with explicit risk ownership and measurable gates.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Compare global versus phased Learning rollout using content readiness, assignment validation, identity access, support coverage, and reporting assurance.
- **SME probe:** Which conditions would cause you to change the recommendation from phased to global, or vice versa?

## 19. Stakeholders disagree on whether the rollout was successful

**Question:** The project team cites on-time delivery, while the business says adoption is weak. How would you define success jointly?

- **Situation:** Delivery milestones and realized business value are being measured separately.
- **Task:** Create a balanced outcome model with shared ownership.
- **Action:** Revisit the original business case; define delivery, service, learner, compliance, and capability measures; establish baselines and targets; assign data and metric owners; agree measurement windows and segmentation; review results after launch and document corrective actions where targets are missed.
- **Result:** Success is judged by both implementation quality and sustained learner/business outcomes.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Combine delivery acceptance with assignment accuracy, successful launch, completion integrity, support demand, and learner feedback.
- **SME probe:** How would you handle a rollout that meets every delivery milestone but fails to improve the intended business outcome?

## 20. You must establish a stakeholder operating model for Learning

**Question:** A global organization has repeated disputes over Learning content, assignment rules, reporting, and support. What stakeholder model would you establish?

- **Situation:** Decisions and ownership are fragmented across business, HR, Compliance, IT, regional teams, and content suppliers.
- **Task:** Establish durable decision rights and operating rhythms.
- **Action:** Map stakeholder groups and their accountabilities; define owners for policy, content, assignment rules, data, integrations, access, support, and reporting; establish decision and escalation forums; maintain decision, risk, and exception logs; agree service measures and review cadence; periodically reassess the model as products and business needs evolve.
- **Result:** Stakeholders know who decides, who executes, who validates, and how unresolved issues are escalated.
- **SAP SuccessFactors example:** Define a Learning governance model covering content lifecycle, assignment policy, compliance evidence, integration changes, security, and service operations.
- **SME probe:** How would you prevent governance forums from becoming approval bottlenecks?

## Candidate self-review checklist

- [ ] Identify stakeholder interests, authority, and accountability.
- [ ] Frame disagreement around the underlying outcome or evidence.
- [ ] Present options and trade-offs without hiding risks.
- [ ] Document decisions, owners, deadlines, and unresolved assumptions.
- [ ] Tailor communication to the audience while maintaining consistent facts.
- [ ] Distinguish consultation from decision authority.
- [ ] Define how adoption and business outcomes will be measured.
- [ ] Preserve trust while escalating material risks transparently.

## Quality bar

A strong answer demonstrates **listen → clarify → align on evidence → present options → secure a decision → communicate → follow through**. Stakeholder management is not merely keeping people informed; it is creating the conditions for timely, accountable decisions.

**Boundary with adjacent themes:** Theme 16 focuses on measurable service and experience improvement. Theme 17 focuses on alignment, decision rights, communication, and commitment across stakeholders.
