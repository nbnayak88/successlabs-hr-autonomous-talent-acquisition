# 11. Rehire Process\n\n> **SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding Interview Mastery Guide**\n> **Lab:** 11 — Scale School of Career Acceleration\n> **Track:** Interview Preparation\n> **Domain:** SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding\n> **Mastery model:** KNOW → DESIGN → DELIVER → SOLVE → INFLUENCE → TRANSFORM\n\n## 1. Objective\n\nMaster how to architect, configure, secure, test, troubleshoot, and govern the **Rehire Process** in SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding.\n\nThe key architectural decision is not simply whether a person is a rehire. It is **which employment model the business requires**:\n\n- Rehire with New Employment\n- Rehire with Old Employment\n- New Hire after a false/incorrect match\n- Cancellation/restart when the rehire decision changes\n\nSAP SuccessFactors Onboarding uses Hire/Rehire Configuration to identify matching inactive employee records. Current SAP documentation describes five matching conditions: first name + last name; first name + date of birth; last name + date of birth; first name + last name + date of birth; and National ID. Eligible matches can include terminated employees, no-show candidates, canceled onboardees for new employment, and active employees with future-dated termination for new-employment scenarios. citeturn0search0\n\n---\n\n## 2. Rehire Architecture at a Glance\n\n```text\nCandidate / New Hire Initiation\n             ↓\n      Pre-Onboarding Rehire Check\n             ↓\n      Potential Match Found?\n          ↙         ↘\n        NO           YES\n        ↓             ↓\n Continue       Profile Match Verification\n Onboarding             ↓\n                 New Hire / Rehire / Cancel\n                         ↓\n              ┌──────────┴──────────┐\n              ↓                     ↓\n      Rehire New Employment   Rehire Old Employment\n              ↓                     ↓\n       New Employment Entity   Reactivate Old Employment\n              ↓                     ↓\n       New User/Employment     Existing Employment\n              ↓                     ↓\n             Onboarding / Hire Completion\n```\n\nSAP documents two rehire verification stages: a pre-onboarding check when onboarding is initiated, and a second Onboarding Rehire Verification after Personal Data Collection if the first check did not identify a match. citeturn0search0\n\n---\n\n## 3. Rehire Verification Stages\n\n### Stage 1 — Pre-Onboarding Rehire Verification\n\nTriggered when onboarding is initiated, before person, employment, or user records are created for the new hire. It uses available candidate information such as name, date of birth, and National ID. citeturn0search0\n\n### Stage 2 — Onboarding Rehire Verification\n\nPerformed after Personal Data Collection when a potential match was not identified earlier. This matters when more complete legal identity information becomes available during onboarding. citeturn0search0\n\n**Architectural principle:** early detection reduces duplicate-person risk; later verification improves match accuracy when richer identity data becomes available.\n\n---\n\n## 4. Rehire with New Employment vs Old Employment\n\n| Dimension | Rehire with New Employment | Rehire with Old Employment |\n|---|---|---|\n| Employment | New employment entity | Previous employment reactivated |\n| Person | Existing person | Existing person |\n| Historical employment | Preserved | Same employment record reused |\n| Current employment | New record | Existing record |\n| User/Employment identifiers | New employment can generate new values | Existing employment context retained |\n| Typical use | New role, changed employment context, legal-entity transfer | Specific repeat/seasonal rehire scenarios |\n| Legal entity transfer | Supported in documented new-employment flow | Not supported |\n\nSAP states that Rehire with New Employment creates a new employment under the existing person details, while Rehire with Old Employment reactivates a previous employment. SAP also notes that Rehire with Old Employment does not support legal-entity transfer and recommends Rehire with New Employment wherever possible. citeturn0search0\n\n---\n\n## 5. Important Identity Model\n\nFor Rehire with New Employment, SAP documents that person-level entities such as Person ID and National ID continue from the previous employment, while employment-level entities such as User ID and Employment ID can be generated again for the new employment. citeturn0search0\n\nConceptually:\n\n```text\nExisting Person\n   ├── Previous Employment\n   │      └── Historical data\n   │\n   └── New Employment\n          └── Current role/data\n```\n\nThis is critical for preserving person identity while separating employment history.\n\n---\n\n# 6. STAR Interview Method\n\nFor every scenario:\n\n- **S — Situation:** business context\n- **T — Task:** architect's responsibility\n- **A — Action:** design/configuration/troubleshooting\n- **R — Result:** measurable or observable outcome\n\nThe strongest answers demonstrate that the candidate understands **identity matching, employment lifecycle, security, legal-entity behavior, and downstream Employee Central impact**.\n\n---\n\n# 7. 20 Deep Scenario-Based Interview Questions & STAR Answers\n\n## Q1. How would you explain the SAP SuccessFactors Onboarding rehire process to a business stakeholder?\n\n**S — Situation:** HR leadership wants to understand why a returning employee should not simply be treated as a brand-new person.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to explain the process without unnecessary technical terminology.\n\n**A — Action:** I explain that Onboarding checks whether the incoming person matches an existing employee record. If a match is found, an authorized participant verifies the record and decides whether to proceed as a new hire, rehire with new employment, rehire with old employment, or cancel. The choice depends on employment history, business policy, and legal/entity requirements.\n\n**R — Result:** HR understands that the process protects person identity and historical employment while preventing duplicate employee records.\n\n**Architect signal:** Rehire is fundamentally an identity and employment-lifecycle decision.\n\n## Q2. What triggers the first rehire check?\n\n**S — Situation:** A customer wants to know when duplicate employee detection occurs.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to explain the timing and data available at that point.\n\n**A — Action:** I explain that Pre-Onboarding Rehire Verification is triggered when onboarding is initiated and occurs before person, employment, or user records are created. It uses available candidate information such as name, date of birth, and National ID.\n\n**R — Result:** The customer understands that potential duplicate identities can be identified before creating a new employee record. SAP documents this pre-onboarding stage explicitly. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q3. Why is there a second rehire check?\n\n**S — Situation:** A former employee is not identified during initial onboarding initiation.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to explain why the system checks again.\n\n**A — Action:** I explain that candidate data may be incomplete at initial initiation. After Personal Data Collection, richer identity information may become available. The second Onboarding Rehire Verification therefore provides another opportunity to identify a previous employee and prevent duplicate person creation.\n\n**R — Result:** The implementation gains a second identity-control point without blocking the initial onboarding experience. SAP documents this two-stage verification model. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q4. A rehire candidate matches an old employee by National ID. What should the coordinator do?\n\n**S — Situation:** The system identifies a National ID match to an inactive employee.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to ensure the match is correctly handled.\n\n**A — Action:** I instruct the authorized rehire coordinator to review the Profile Match Verification result and validate the identity. I do not allow the user to bypass the match simply to accelerate onboarding. If the person is genuinely the same individual, I select the appropriate rehire path based on employment requirements.\n\n**R — Result:** The organization avoids duplicate person creation and preserves the correct historical identity. SAP notes that the Proceed as New Hire option is grayed out when a National ID match is detected during the relevant verification. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q5. When would you choose Rehire with New Employment?\n\n**S — Situation:** A former employee returns for a substantially different employment arrangement.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to select the correct employment model.\n\n**A — Action:** I evaluate whether the person should retain the same person identity while receiving a distinct new employment record. I consider role, employment dates, legal entity, payroll, organizational structure, security, reporting, and historical employment requirements.\n\n**R — Result:** A new employment is created under the existing person, keeping employment history distinct. SAP documents this as the Rehire with New Employment model. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q6. When would you choose Rehire with Old Employment?\n\n**S — Situation:** A retailer frequently rehiring seasonal workers wants to reactivate previous employment records.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to determine whether the old-employment model fits the business.\n\n**A — Action:** I assess whether the business genuinely needs the previous employment record reactivated rather than creating a new employment. I validate contract, seniority, payroll, reporting, and historical-data requirements. I also document the limitation that legal-entity transfer is not supported by this model.\n\n**R — Result:** The organization uses old employment only where the lifecycle and business requirements justify it. SAP cites seasonal rehire as a potential use case and recommends new employment where possible. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q7. A business wants to use Rehire with Old Employment for a legal-entity transfer. What do you advise?\n\n**S — Situation:** An employee is moving from one company/legal entity to another and the project team proposes old-employment rehire.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to prevent an unsupported design.\n\n**A — Action:** I explain that SAP currently does not support legal-entity transfer with Rehire with Old Employment. I redesign the scenario using Rehire with New Employment, subject to the required termination/rehire dates and permissions.\n\n**R — Result:** The solution follows a supported employment model and avoids forcing an unsupported configuration. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q8. How would you design rehire matching rules?\n\n**S — Situation:** A customer has duplicate records caused by inconsistent names and missing identity data.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to improve match accuracy while controlling false positives.\n\n**A — Action:** I review the Hire/Rehire Configuration and select appropriate matching conditions. SAP currently documents five conditions: first name + last name; first name + date of birth; last name + date of birth; first name + last name + date of birth; and National ID. I prioritize stronger identity attributes where appropriate and test both false-positive and false-negative scenarios.\n\n**R — Result:** Match quality improves while the rehire coordinator receives manageable verification results. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q9. The system identifies the wrong person as a potential rehire. How do you troubleshoot?\n\n**S — Situation:** A new candidate shares a common name with an inactive employee.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to prevent a false match from becoming a rehire.\n\n**A — Action:** I inspect which matching condition produced the candidate, compare available identity data, and validate the Profile Match Verification result. I avoid broadening permissions or forcing the user into a rehire path. I refine matching conditions or improve source data where the root cause is data quality.\n\n**R — Result:** False positives are handled through evidence-based verification instead of creating incorrect employment relationships.\n\n## Q10. A former employee is rehired but the implementation creates a duplicate person. What is your approach?\n\n**S — Situation:** A returning employee now has two person records.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to determine why rehire matching failed.\n\n**A — Action:** I trace the lifecycle from Recruiting/ATS data through the first rehire check, Personal Data Collection, second rehire check, and final hire. I inspect whether required identity fields were missing or inconsistent, whether matching rules were configured correctly, and whether a participant incorrectly selected New Hire.\n\n**R — Result:** The root cause is identified and the implementation can improve both data completeness and rehire controls.\n\n## Q11. How would you secure the Profile Match Verification process?\n\n**S — Situation:** HR wants hiring managers to verify rehire matches.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to avoid exposing previous employee data unnecessarily.\n\n**A — Action:** I create dedicated rehire permissions and limit access to the appropriate rehire coordinators. I validate target population, employee-data permissions, and the exact actions each persona can perform. Current SAP 1H 2026 functionality can enforce target-population restrictions for Rehire Inactive Employee and Rehire Inactive Employee with New Employment permissions. citeturn0search5\n\n**R — Result:** Authorized users can perform rehire decisions while access to historical employee information is constrained.\n\n## Q12. How would you handle a future-dated termination followed by rehire?\n\n**S — Situation:** An active employee has a future termination date and is scheduled to return under a new employment.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to ensure the timing does not create conflicting employment records.\n\n**A — Action:** I validate the termination and rehire dates, complete required onboarding data-collection and review steps before the termination date, and ensure the rehire is modeled as new employment where required.\n\n**R — Result:** The transition is aligned to the employment lifecycle and avoids overlapping active employment conflicts. SAP documents this future-dated termination scenario for Rehire with New Employment. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q13. How would you design a legal-entity transfer using Rehire with New Employment?\n\n**S — Situation:** An employee transfers from one legal entity to another, potentially across countries.\n\n**T — Task:** I need to design the lifecycle without corrupting historical employment data.\n\n**A — Action:** I plan a controlled termination and new employment. SAP documents that the employee should have a future termination date and the rehire start date should be the day after termination. I also validate the required rehire permissions and integration path. For this scenario, SAP notes that legal-entity transfer with Rehire with New Employment supports external ATS integration but is not supported when onboarding is initiated manually.\n\n**R — Result:** Historical employment remains distinct while the new legal-entity employment is created through a supported flow. citeturn0search0\n\n## Q14. A rehire is stuck in Data Review. A candidate was a no-show previously and is now returning. SAP currently includes no-show candidates among records eligible to appear in Profile Match Verification.
